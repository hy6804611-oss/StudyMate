🏛 GOVERNMENT

Government is the study of the institutions, principles, processes and structures through which a state is governed. It explains how leaders are chosen, how laws are made, the rights and duties of citizens, political systems and the relationship between the government and the people. Government helps students understand democracy, leadership, citizenship and national development.

🏛 CHAPTER 2: ORIGIN OF THE STATE

The origin of the state explains how organized societies came into existence. Over the years, political philosophers have developed different theories to explain how states were formed and why governments exist.

Meaning of the State

A state is a politically organized body of people occupying a definite territory, governed by a recognized government and possessing sovereignty.

Features of a State

Population – A state must have people living within it.

Territory – A state must have clearly defined geographical boundaries.

Government – A state must have a government to make and enforce laws.

Sovereignty – A state must possess supreme authority over its internal and external affairs.

Theories of the Origin of the State

Divine Right Theory – This theory states that the state was established by God and that rulers derive their authority from God. Therefore, citizens are expected to obey the ruler.

Force Theory – This theory explains that the state was formed when a strong individual or group used force to conquer and control weaker people.

Social Contract Theory – This theory states that people voluntarily agreed to surrender some of their rights to a government in exchange for peace, security and protection. Philosophers associated with this theory include Thomas Hobbes, John Locke and Jean-Jacques Rousseau.

Evolutionary Theory – This theory explains that the state developed gradually from simple family units to clans, tribes, communities and finally modern states.

Importance of the State

• Maintains peace and order.

• Protects lives and property.

• Promotes economic development.

• Provides essential social services.

• Protects the rights of citizens.

• Promotes national unity.

⭐ Learn the four theories of the origin of the state.

🏛 CHAPTER 5: SYSTEMS OF GOVERNMENT

A system of government refers to the way political power is distributed among the different levels of government within a country. The major systems of government are Unitary, Federal and Confederal systems.

Unitary System of Government

A unitary system of government is one in which all governmental powers are concentrated in a single central government. Local authorities exist mainly to carry out the instructions of the central government.

Features of a Unitary System

Centralized Power – The central government holds supreme authority.

Single – The country operates under one .

Single Legislature – There is usually one national parliament.

Uniform Laws – The same laws apply throughout the country.

Advantages of a Unitary System

• Simple and easy to administer.

• Quick decision-making.

• Less expensive to operate.

• Promotes national unity.

Disadvantages of a Unitary System

• Too much power is concentrated at the centre.

• Local needs may be ignored.

• It may encourage dictatorship.

Federal System of Government

A federal system of government is one in which governmental powers are ally shared between the central (federal) government and the state or regional governments.

Features of a Federal System

Written – Powers are clearly divided by the .

Division of Powers – Both federal and state governments have al powers.

Independent Judiciary – Courts interpret the and settle disputes.

Advantages of a Federal System

Disadvantages of a Federal System

• Expensive to operate.

• May lead to conflicts between federal and state governments.

• Duplication of government functions may occur.

🏛 CHAPTER 6: THE ARMS OF GOVERNMENT

The arms of government are the three major divisions responsible for making, implementing and interpreting the laws of a country. They work together to ensure effective governance while preventing the abuse of power.

The Legislature

The Legislature is the arm of government responsible for making laws. It represents the interests of the people and oversees the activities of the Executive.

Functions of the Legislature

• Makes laws for the country.

• Amends existing laws.

• Approves the national budget.

• Monitors and investigates government activities.

• Confirms certain government appointments where required.

The Executive

The Executive is the arm of government responsible for implementing and enforcing laws. It is headed by the President in a presidential system or the Prime Minister in a parliamentary system.

Functions of the Executive

• Implements laws passed by the Legislature.

• Maintains law and order.

• Directs national policies.

• Defends the country.

• Prepares the national budget.

The Judiciary

The Judiciary is the arm of government responsible for interpreting laws and administering justice. Judges and magistrates settle disputes according to the and the law.

Functions of the Judiciary

• Interprets the .

• Settles disputes between individuals, organizations and governments.

• Protects the rights of citizens.

• Punishes lawbreakers according to the law.

• Ensures justice is fairly administered.

Separation of Powers

Separation of powers is the principle by which governmental powers are divided among the Legislature, Executive and Judiciary. This prevents any one arm from becoming too powerful and promotes checks and balances.

Importance of Separation of Powers

• Prevents abuse of power.

• Promotes accountability.

• Protects democracy.

• Ensures the independence of each arm of government.

• Promotes good governance and the rule of law.

🏛 CHAPTER 7: POLITICAL PARTIES AND PARTY SYSTEMS

A political party is an organized group of people who share similar political ideas and work together to gain political power through elections. Political parties present candidates for public office and seek to influence government policies.

Meaning of a Political Party

A political party is an association of citizens with common political beliefs and objectives who seek to form or control the government through al means.

Functions of Political Parties

• Educate and enlighten citizens on political issues.

• Form and control the government after winning elections.

• Provide opposition to check the activities of the ruling party.

• Promote national unity by bringing people together.

• Develop policies and programmes for national development.

Characteristics of Political Parties

Common Ideology – Members share similar political beliefs and goals.

Leadership – Every political party has recognized leaders.

Membership – Citizens voluntarily join political parties.

Manifesto – Political parties prepare a manifesto containing their plans and promises to the people.

Party Systems

A party system refers to the number of political parties that effectively operate within a country's political system.

One-Party System

A one-party system is a political system in which only one political party is legally allowed to exist and govern the country.

Advantages

• Promotes political stability.

• Speeds up decision-making.

• Reduces political conflicts.

Disadvantages

• Encourages dictatorship.

• Limits political freedom.

• Citizens have little or no choice during elections.

Two-Party System

A two-party system is one in which two major political parties dominate elections and government, although smaller parties may still exist.

Advantages

• Provides a strong opposition.

• Promotes political stability.

• Makes government more accountable.

Disadvantages

• Smaller parties have little chance of winning.

• Voters have fewer major choices.

Multi-Party System

A multi-party system is one in which several political parties compete for political power through elections.

Advantages

• Gives citizens more political choices.

• Encourages democratic participation.

• Represents different interests and opinions.

Disadvantages

• Coalition governments may be unstable.

• Decision-making may be slower.

🏛 CHAPTER 8: THE ELECTORAL PROCESS

The electoral process refers to the series of activities involved in choosing leaders through voting in a free and fair election. It includes voter registration, nomination of candidates, campaigns, voting, counting of votes, declaration of results and, where necessary, the resolution of election disputes.

Meaning of an Election

An election is the process through which eligible citizens vote to choose individuals who will represent them or govern the country for a specified period.

Importance of Elections

• Allows citizens to choose their leaders.

• Promotes democracy and good governance.

• Ensures peaceful transfer of political power.

• Makes leaders accountable to the people.

• Gives legitimacy to governments.

Stages of the Electoral Process

Voter Registration – Eligible citizens register so they can vote during elections.

Nomination of Candidates – Political parties select candidates to contest elections.

Election Campaigns – Candidates present their programmes and seek public support.

Voting – Registered voters cast their votes for candidates of their choice.

Counting of Votes – Votes are counted after voting ends.

Declaration of Results – The electoral body announces the winners.

Election Petitions – Aggrieved candidates may challenge election results in court or election tribunals.

Qualities of a Free and Fair Election

• Free from intimidation and violence.

• Conducted according to the law.

• Secret voting is guaranteed.

• Equal opportunity for all political parties.

• Transparent counting and announcement of results.

Electoral Malpractices

Electoral malpractice refers to illegal actions that undermine the credibility of elections.

Examples include:

• Vote buying.

• Ballot box snatching.

• Multiple voting.

• Underage voting.

• Voter intimidation.

• Falsification of election results.

Ways to Prevent Electoral Malpractices

• Enforce electoral laws strictly.

• Educate voters on their rights and responsibilities.

• Ensure an independent electoral commission.

• Punish electoral offenders.

• Improve election security and transparency.

Exam Tips

⭐ Differentiate between free and fair elections and electoral malpractice.

🏛 CHAPTER 10: CITIZENSHIP

Citizenship is the legal relationship between an individual and a country. A citizen enjoys certain rights, privileges and protections from the government while also performing responsibilities and obeying the laws of the country.

Meaning of Citizenship

Citizenship is the legal status of belonging to a country, which gives a person the right to participate fully in the political, social and economic life of that country.

Ways of Acquiring Citizenship

Citizenship by Birth

A person becomes a citizen because they are born in a particular country or are born to parents who are citizens of that country.

Citizenship by Registration

Citizenship granted to a foreigner after meeting legal requirements, such as marriage to a citizen or fulfilling conditions set by the government.

Citizenship by Naturalization

Citizenship granted to a foreign national who has lived in a country for a specified period, demonstrated good character, and satisfied all legal requirements.

Citizenship by Descent

A person acquires citizenship through his or her parents or ancestors, even if born outside the country.

Rights of Citizens

• Right to life.

• Right to personal liberty.

• Freedom of expression.

• Freedom of religion.

• Right to vote and be voted for (where eligible).

• Right to own property.

• Right to fair hearing.

• Freedom of movement.

Duties and Responsibilities of Citizens

• Obey the laws of the country.

• Pay taxes honestly.

• Respect national symbols such as the flag and anthem.

• Protect public property.

• Vote responsibly during elections.

• Defend the country when required by law.

• Promote peace, unity and national development.

Importance of Good Citizenship

• Promotes national unity.

• Encourages peaceful coexistence.

• Supports democratic governance.

• Contributes to economic and social development.

• Strengthens national security.

🏛 CHAPTER 11: FUNDAMENTAL HUMAN RIGHTS

Fundamental Human Rights are the basic rights and freedoms that belong to every human being by virtue of being human. These rights are guaranteed and protected by the and are essential for the dignity, freedom and development of individuals.

Meaning of Fundamental Human Rights

Fundamental Human Rights are basic legal rights that every citizen is entitled to enjoy without discrimination based on race, religion, ethnicity, gender or political opinion.

Major Fundamental Human Rights

Right to Life – Every person has the right to live and cannot be deprived of life unlawfully.

Right to Personal Liberty – No one should be arrested or detained unlawfully.

Right to Fair Hearing – Every accused person has the right to a fair and impartial trial.

Freedom of Expression – Citizens have the right to express their opinions freely, provided they do not violate the law.

Freedom of Thought, Conscience and Religion – Individuals are free to choose and practise any religion or belief.

Freedom of Movement – Citizens have the right to move freely within the country and reside in any part of it.

Freedom of Association – Citizens may join political parties, trade unions, clubs and other lawful organizations.

Right to Privacy – Individuals have the right to the privacy of their homes, correspondence and communications.

Right to Own Property – Citizens have the right to acquire and own property in accordance with the law.

Limitations of Human Rights

Fundamental rights are not absolute. They may be restricted in situations involving national security, public safety, public health, public morality, public order or the protection of the rights of others.

Importance of Fundamental Human Rights

• Protect human dignity.

• Promote equality before the law.

• Prevent oppression and abuse of power.

• Encourage democracy and justice.

• Promote peace and social stability.

Responsibilities of Citizens While Enjoying Their Rights

• Respect the rights of others.

• Obey the laws of the country.

• Protect public property.

• Promote peace and national unity.

• Report violations of the law to the appropriate authorities.

💰 CHAPTER 17: PUBLIC FINANCE

Meaning of Public Finance

Public Finance is the branch of government that deals with the raising, management and spending of public funds to provide goods and services for the benefit of the people.

Sources of Public Revenue

Taxation – Money collected from individuals and businesses by the government.

Borrowing – Loans obtained from local or foreign sources.

Grants and Aid – Financial assistance from foreign governments and international organizations.

Customs and Excise Duties – Taxes on imported, exported and locally manufactured goods.

Fees and Licences – Revenue from issuing licences and government services.

Fines and Penalties – Money paid for breaking laws or regulations.

Profits from Government Enterprises – Income generated by government-owned companies.

Government Expenditure

• Payment of workers' salaries and wages.

• Construction and maintenance of roads.

• Provision of education and healthcare.

• Defence and national security.

• Agricultural and industrial development.

• Payment of pensions and public debt.

Types of Government Expenditure

Capital Expenditure – Spending on long-term projects such as roads, bridges, schools and hospitals.

Recurrent Expenditure – Spending on daily government operations such as salaries, electricity and maintenance.

Budget

A budget is an estimate of the government's expected revenue and expenditure for a specific financial year.

Importance of Public Finance

• Promotes national development.

• Provides public goods and services.

• Creates employment opportunities.

• Maintains law and order.

• Reduces poverty through social programmes.

• Promotes economic growth and stability.

Problems of Public Finance

• Corruption and embezzlement.

• Poor tax collection.

• Excessive public debt.

• Budget deficits.

• Mismanagement of public funds.

Exam Tips

⭐ Know the meaning of Public Finance.

⭐ Learn the sources of government revenue.

⭐ Differentiate between capital and recurrent expenditure.

⭐ Understand the meaning and purpose of a budget.

⭐ Know the importance and problems of public finance.

📰 CHAPTER 18: PUBLIC OPINION

Public opinion refers to the views, beliefs, attitudes and judgments of the majority of people on issues affecting society and government. In a democratic society, public opinion plays an important role in influencing government policies and decision-making.

Meaning of Public Opinion

Public opinion is the collective opinion or judgment of citizens on political, economic and social matters affecting their country.

Characteristics of Public Opinion

Majority View – It reflects the opinion of a large number of people.

Dynamic – It changes over time as new information becomes available.

Influential – It can influence government policies and leaders.

Issue-Based – It focuses on matters affecting society.

Expressed Freely – Citizens should be able to express their opinions without fear.

Importance of Public Opinion

• Guides government in making policies.

• Promotes accountability in government.

• Strengthens democracy.

• Protects citizens' interests.

• Encourages good governance.

• Helps leaders understand the needs of the people.

Factors That Influence Public Opinion

• Education.

• Religion.

• Culture and traditions.

• Family background.

• Political parties.

• The mass media.

• Social media.

• Economic conditions.

How Public Opinion is Measured

• Opinion polls.

• Public debates.

• Elections.

• Referendums.

• Questionnaires and surveys.

Limitations of Public Opinion

• False information may influence people.

• Some citizens may not express their true opinions.

• Illiteracy can affect understanding of national issues.

• Public opinion may change rapidly.

Exam Tips

⭐ Know the meaning of public opinion.

⭐ Memorize the importance of public opinion.

⭐ Learn the factors that influence public opinion.

⭐ Understand how public opinion is measured.

⭐ Be able to explain the role of public opinion in a democratic society.

📰 CHAPTER 19: POLITICAL PARTIES

A political party is an organized group of people who share similar political ideas and seek to gain and exercise political power through elections and other lawful means. Political parties are essential institutions in every democratic system because they provide candidates for public office and help citizens participate in governance.

Meaning of a Political Party

A political party is an association of people with common political beliefs and objectives, formed to contest elections and control government.

Features of Political Parties

Common Ideology – Members share similar political beliefs and goals.

Leadership – Every political party has recognized leaders.

Membership – Citizens are free to join political parties of their choice.

Manifesto – Every party presents its programmes and promises to the people.

Contesting Elections – Political parties nominate candidates for elections.

Functions of Political Parties

• Educate citizens on political issues.

• Present candidates for elections.

• Form government after winning elections.

• Provide opposition to the ruling government.

• Promote political participation.

• Develop future political leaders.

• Help maintain democratic governance.

Types of Party Systems

One-Party System – Only one political party is legally allowed to operate.

Two-Party System – Two major political parties dominate elections and governance.

Multi-Party System – Several political parties compete for political power.

Advantages of a Multi-Party System

• Encourages democracy.

• Gives citizens more choices.

• Prevents dictatorship.

• Promotes healthy political competition.

Disadvantages of a Multi-Party System

• May lead to unstable coalition governments.

• Can increase political conflicts.

• Elections are often expensive.

• Too many parties may confuse voters.

Exam Tips

⭐ Know the meaning of a political party.

⭐ Memorize the functions of political parties.

⭐ Understand the different party systems.

⭐ Learn the advantages and disadvantages of the multi-party system.

⭐ Be able to explain the importance of political parties in democracy.

📰 CHAPTER 20: ELECTORAL SYSTEM AND ELECTIONS

An election is the process through which eligible citizens choose their leaders by voting. The electoral system consists of the laws, rules and procedures used to conduct free, fair and credible elections in a democratic society.

Meaning of Election

An election is the process of selecting leaders through voting by qualified citizens.

Meaning of Electoral System

An electoral system is the set of rules and procedures used to organize, conduct and supervise elections.

Types of Elections

General Election – Conducted to elect leaders into various public offices across the country.

By-Election – Held to fill a vacant political office before the end of its tenure.

Primary Election – Conducted by political parties to select their candidates.

Run-off Election – Held when no candidate obtains the required majority of votes.

Features of a Free and Fair Election

• Free participation of eligible voters.

• Secret ballot.

• Independent electoral body.

• Equal opportunity for political parties.

• Transparent vote counting.

• Absence of intimidation and violence.

Functions of an Electoral Commission

• Registers eligible voters.

• Conducts elections.

• Registers political parties.

• Announces election results.

• Educates citizens on voting procedures.

• Monitors compliance with electoral laws.

Electoral Malpractices

Ballot Box Snatching – Stealing or destroying ballot boxes during elections.

Vote Buying – Offering money or gifts to influence voters.

Rigging – Manipulating election results illegally.

Multiple Voting – Voting more than once in the same election.

Voter Intimidation – Threatening voters to influence their choices.

Ways to Prevent Electoral Malpractices

• Strict enforcement of electoral laws.

• Civic education for voters.

• Independent electoral commission.

• Use of secure election technology.

• Punishment of electoral offenders.

Exam Tips

⭐ Know the meaning of election and electoral system.

⭐ Understand the different types of elections.

⭐ Memorize the functions of the electoral commission.

⭐ Learn the features of a free and fair election.

⭐ Be able to identify common electoral malpractices and their solutions.

🏛️ CHAPTER 21: ELECTORAL COMMISSION

Meaning of an Electoral Commission

An Electoral Commission is an independent body established by law to organize, supervise, and conduct free, fair, and credible elections in a country.

Functions of an Electoral Commission

Conducting Elections – Organizes national, state, and local government elections.

Voter Registration – Registers eligible citizens as voters.

Political Party Registration – Registers qualified political parties according to the law.

Monitoring Political Parties – Ensures political parties obey electoral laws.

Announcing Election Results – Declares official election results after counting votes.

Voter Education – Educates citizens on voting procedures and civic responsibilities.

Preparation of Election Materials – Produces ballot papers, voter registers, and other election materials.

Qualities of a Good Electoral Commission

• Independence from political influence.

• Transparency in all electoral activities.

• Fairness to all political parties.

• Honesty and integrity.

• Professional competence.

• Accountability.

Importance of an Electoral Commission

• Promotes democracy.

• Ensures peaceful transfer of power.

• Protects citizens' voting rights.

• Reduces electoral fraud.

• Increases public confidence in elections.

Exam Tips

⭐ Know the meaning of an Electoral Commission.

⭐ Study its major functions.

⭐ Learn the qualities of a credible Electoral Commission.

⭐ Understand its importance in sustaining democracy.

🌍 CHAPTER 22: INTERNATIONAL ORGANIZATIONS

International organizations are associations of sovereign countries formed to promote cooperation, peace, security, economic development, and mutual understanding among member states. These organizations help countries work together to solve global and regional problems that no single nation can solve alone.

Meaning of International Organizations

An international organization is a formal association of independent countries established by agreement to achieve common political, economic, social, cultural, or security objectives.

Reasons for Establishing International Organizations

• To promote international peace and security.

• To encourage economic cooperation.

• To improve trade among nations.

• To settle disputes peacefully.

• To promote human rights.

• To encourage scientific, educational and technological development.

• To provide humanitarian assistance during emergencies.

Major International Organizations

United Nations (UN) – Established in 1945 to maintain international peace and security.

African Union (AU) – Established in 2002 to promote unity and development among African countries.

Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) – Established in 1975 to promote economic integration and peace in West Africa.

Commonwealth of Nations – Promotes cooperation among member countries, many of which were formerly under British rule.

Organization of Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) – Coordinates petroleum policies among oil-producing countries.

Importance of International Organizations

• Promote world peace and security.

• Encourage cooperation among nations.

• Improve international trade.

• Promote economic and social development.

• Protect human rights.

• Provide humanitarian assistance.

• Help settle international disputes peacefully.

• Encourage education, science and technology.

Challenges Facing International Organizations

• Lack of adequate funding.

• Political disagreements among member states.

• Conflicts between powerful nations.

• Failure of some countries to obey decisions.

• Slow decision-making process.

Exam Tips

⭐ Know the meaning of international organizations.

⭐ Memorize the major international organizations and their objectives.

⭐ Learn why countries join international organizations.

⭐ Understand the importance and challenges of international organizations.

⭐ Be able to distinguish between global organizations (e.g. UN) and regional organizations (e.g. AU and ECOWAS).

🌍 CHAPTER 23: FOREIGN POLICY

Meaning of Foreign Policy

Foreign Policy refers to the principles, objectives and actions that guide a country's relationship with other countries and international organizations. It helps a nation protect its interests and promote peace, security and development.

Objectives of Nigeria's Foreign Policy

• To protect Nigeria's national interest.

• To promote peace and friendly relations with other nations.

• To encourage economic growth and international trade.

• To protect the sovereignty and territorial integrity of Nigeria.

• To promote African unity and cooperation.

• To support world peace and international security.

Principles of Nigeria's Foreign Policy

Africa as the Centrepiece – gives priority to African affairs.

Respect for Sovereignty – respects the independence of other nations.

Non-Interference – does not interfere in the internal affairs of other countries.

Peaceful Settlement of Disputes – supports dialogue and negotiation instead of war.

International Cooperation – works with other countries to achieve common goals.

Factors Influencing Foreign Policy

• National interest.

• Economic resources.

• Political stability.

• Public opinion.

• International law.

• Membership of international organizations.

Importance of Foreign Policy

• Promotes peaceful relations among nations.

• Encourages international trade and investment.

• Protects citizens living abroad.

• Improves national security.

• Enhances Nigeria's image in the international community.

🏘️ CHAPTER 24: LOCAL GOVERNMENT

Meaning of Local Government

Local Government is the third tier of government established by law to administer the affairs of people at the grassroots level. It is responsible for providing basic services and promoting development within local communities.

Functions of Local Government

• Construction and maintenance of local roads.

• Collection of rates, taxes and licences.

• Registration of births, deaths and marriages.

• Provision and maintenance of markets and motor parks.

• Environmental sanitation and waste disposal.

• Provision of primary education and primary healthcare.

• Maintenance of public cemeteries and recreational centres.

• Promotion of agriculture and rural development.

Sources of Revenue of Local Government

Rates – Property and tenement rates.

Taxes – Community and local taxes.

Licences – Fees collected from permits and licences.

Fines – Revenue from penalties imposed by law.

Grants – Financial assistance from state and federal governments.

Federal Allocation – Statutory allocation from the Federation Account.

Importance of Local Government

• Brings government closer to the people.

• Promotes grassroots development.

• Encourages local participation in governance.

• Creates employment opportunities.

• Provides essential social amenities.

Problems of Local Government

• Inadequate funding.

• Corruption and mismanagement.

• Political interference.

• Lack of qualified personnel.

• Poor planning and administration.

• Inadequate infrastructure.

Ways of Improving Local Government

• Ensure financial autonomy.

• Employ qualified personnel.

• Improve transparency and accountability.

• Increase government allocation.

• Encourage citizens' participation.

Exam Tips

⭐ Learn the functions of Local Government.

⭐ Memorize the sources of Local Government revenue.

⭐ Understand the problems facing Local Government.

👨‍💼 CHAPTER 25: CIVIL SERVICE

Meaning of Civil Service

The Civil Service is the permanent administrative body of government made up of public servants who assist in implementing government policies and programmes. Civil servants are appointed based on merit and continue in office regardless of changes in government.

Characteristics of the Civil Service

Permanence – Civil servants remain in office even when governments change.

Neutrality – They remain politically impartial and serve every lawful government.

Anonymity – Civil servants work behind the scenes while ministers take public responsibility.

Hierarchy – The Civil Service has different ranks and levels of authority.

Professionalism – Officers are expected to possess the required knowledge and skills.

Functions of the Civil Service

• Advises government on policy matters.

• Implements government policies and programmes.

• Maintains government records and official documents.

• Collects and manages government revenue.

• Provides essential public services.

• Assists in preparing the national budget.

• Ensures continuity in government administration.

Problems of the Civil Service

• Corruption and bribery.

• Bureaucratic delays (red tapism).

• Political interference.

• Inadequate funding.

• Poor motivation of workers.

• Lack of modern equipment and technology.

Ways of Improving the Civil Service

• Recruit qualified personnel.

• Improve workers' salaries and welfare.

• Provide regular training and retraining.

• Promote transparency and accountability.

• Introduce modern technology into government offices.

• Enforce discipline and punish corruption.

Exam Tips

⭐ Memorize the characteristics of the Civil Service.

⭐ Learn the functions of the Civil Service.

⭐ Know the ways of improving the Civil Service.

🏛️ CHAPTER 26: PUBLIC CORPORATIONS

Meaning of Public Corporation

A Public Corporation is a government-owned business enterprise established by an Act of Parliament to provide essential goods and services to the public. It has a separate legal identity and is managed by a board appointed by the government.

Characteristics of Public Corporations

Government Ownership – They are wholly or partly owned by the government.

Legal Entity – They can sue and be sued in their own name.

Perpetual Succession – They continue to exist despite changes in government.

Public Service – Their main objective is to provide services rather than maximize profit.

Board Management – They are managed by a board of directors appointed by the government.

Functions of Public Corporations

• Provide essential public services.

• Promote economic development.

• Create employment opportunities.

• Supply strategic goods and services.

• Generate revenue for the government.

• Promote industrial and technological growth.

Advantages of Public Corporations

• Provide affordable services to citizens.

• Promote national development.

• Reduce monopoly by private companies.

• Encourage balanced regional development.

• Create jobs for the population.

Problems of Public Corporations

• Corruption and mismanagement.

• Political interference.

• Poor funding.

• Inefficiency and bureaucracy.

• Poor maintenance of facilities.

• Low productivity.

Ways of Improving Public Corporations

• Appoint qualified managers.

• Reduce political interference.

• Improve supervision and accountability.

• Ensure proper funding.

• Introduce modern technology and efficient management.

Exam Tips

⭐ Learn the characteristics of Public Corporations.

⭐ Memorize their functions.
