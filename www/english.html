📘 ENGLISH LANGUAGE

English Language is one of the most widely spoken and studied languages in the world. It serves as an international language for communication, education, science, technology, business, diplomacy and travel. Learning English helps individuals communicate effectively, express ideas clearly, understand written and spoken information, and interact confidently with people from different cultures and countries. A strong foundation in English also improves reading, writing, listening, speaking and critical thinking skills that are essential for academic success and everyday life.

📘 CHAPTER 1: PARTS OF SPEECH

Meaning of Parts of Speech

Parts of speech are the different categories into which words are classified according to the functions they perform in a sentence. Every sentence is made up of words, and each word has a specific role. Understanding the parts of speech helps learners construct meaningful sentences, communicate accurately, and improve both spoken and written English.

The English language has eight major parts of speech. Although each part performs a different function, they work together to form complete and meaningful sentences.

Importance of Parts of Speech

Improves Communication – Understanding the parts of speech helps people communicate ideas clearly and correctly in both speaking and writing.

Develops Grammar Skills – Learning the different classes of words makes it easier to understand grammar rules and sentence construction.

Enhances Reading and Writing – Readers can understand texts more easily, while writers can create clear, accurate and meaningful sentences.

Builds Vocabulary – Recognising how words function in sentences helps learners expand and use vocabulary effectively.

Supports Academic Success – A good understanding of parts of speech is essential for success in English examinations and other school subjects.

The Eight Parts of Speech

The eight major parts of speech are:

• Nouns

• Pronouns

• Verbs

• Adjectives

• Adverbs

• Prepositions

• Conjunctions

• Interjections

1. Nouns

A noun is a word used to name a person, place, animal, object, event or idea. Nouns are among the most important words in the English language because they identify the people, places, things and concepts we talk about every day.

Examples of Nouns

• Person – teacher, doctor, engineer, student

• Place – school, city, library, hospital

• Animal – elephant, tiger, dolphin, eagle

• Object – computer, table, pencil, bicycle

• Idea – honesty, freedom, happiness, courage

Examples in Sentences

• The teacher explained the lesson clearly.

• We visited the museum during our holiday.

• Honesty is an important personal value.

• The computer is connected to the internet.

Types of Nouns

Common Nouns – These are general names of people, places, animals or things.

Examples: city, book, river, child.

Proper Nouns – These are specific names of people, places, organisations or countries. Proper nouns always begin with a capital letter.

Examples: Maria, Canada, Amazon, Mount Everest.

Concrete Nouns – These are things that can be seen, touched, heard, smelled or tasted.

Examples: apple, guitar, flower, dog.

Abstract Nouns – These are ideas, qualities or feelings that cannot be touched or seen.

Examples: kindness, wisdom, peace, love.

Collective Nouns – These refer to a group of people, animals or things treated as one unit.

Examples: team, family, audience, flock.

Countable Nouns – These can be counted individually.

Examples: chair, pencil, car.

Uncountable Nouns – These cannot usually be counted individually.

Examples: water, milk, information, furniture.

📘 CHAPTER 2: PRONOUN

A pronoun is a word that is used in place of a noun. Pronouns help avoid unnecessary repetition and make sentences smoother, shorter and easier to understand. Instead of repeating the same noun several times, a suitable pronoun can be used.

Examples of Pronouns

• I, you, he, she, it, we, they

• me, him, her, us, them

• mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs

Examples in Sentences

• David is a doctor. He works at a hospital.

• Maria enjoys reading. She visits the library every weekend.

• The students completed their assignments before the deadline.

• The laptop belongs to me. It is mine.

Types of Pronouns

Personal Pronouns – These refer to specific people or things.

Examples: I, you, he, she, it, we, they.

Possessive Pronouns – These show ownership or possession.

Examples: mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs.

Reflexive Pronouns – These refer back to the subject of a sentence.

Examples: myself, yourself, himself, herself, ourselves, themselves.

Demonstrative Pronouns – These point to specific people or things.

Examples: this, that, these, those.

Interrogative Pronouns – These are used to ask questions.

Examples: who, whom, whose, which, what.

Relative Pronouns – These connect a clause to a noun.

Examples: who, whom, whose, which, that.

Indefinite Pronouns – These refer to people or things without identifying them specifically.

Examples: everyone, somebody, anybody, nobody, several, many.

Common Errors in the Use of Pronouns

Incorrect: Sarah lost Sarah's bag because Sarah was careless.

Correct: Sarah lost her bag because she was careless.

Incorrect: Me and John are classmates.

Correct: John and I are classmates.

Incorrect: The responsibility belongs to John and I.

Correct: The responsibility belongs to John and me.

Pronoun Agreement

A pronoun must agree with the noun it replaces in number, gender and person.

Examples

• Every student should submit his or her assignment on time.

• The children finished their homework.

• My sister said she would arrive early.

Exam Tips

⭐ Know the different types of pronouns and their functions.

⭐ Ensure that every pronoun agrees with the noun it replaces.

⭐ Avoid unnecessary repetition of nouns by using suitable pronouns.

⭐ Learn the difference between subject pronouns (I, he, she, we, they) and object pronouns (me, him, her, us, them).

⭐ Practise identifying pronouns in passages and sentences.

📘 CHAPTER 3: VERB

A verb is a word that expresses an action, an occurrence or a state of being. It is one of the most important parts of a sentence because every complete sentence must contain at least one verb. Verbs tell us what a person, animal or thing does, what happens, or what condition exists.

Without a verb, a sentence cannot express a complete thought. For this reason, the verb is often described as the "heart" of a sentence.

Examples of Verbs

• Action Verbs – run, write, swim, jump, read, sing, drive

• State Verbs – be, seem, know, believe, belong, love, understand

Examples in Sentences

• The children played in the park.

• She writes beautiful stories.

• They are excited about the trip.

• We believe in honesty and hard work.

Types of Verbs

Action Verbs – These describe physical or mental actions performed by the subject.

Examples: walk, cook, think, laugh, paint.

Linking Verbs – These connect the subject with more information rather than showing action.

Examples: is, am, are, was, were, become, seem, appear.

Helping (Auxiliary) Verbs – These work together with the main verb to express tense, mood or voice.

Examples: have, has, had, do, does, did, will, shall, can, could, may, might, should, must.

Examples in Sentences

• She has finished her homework.

• They will travel tomorrow.

• He can solve the problem quickly.

Verb Tenses

Tense shows the time at which an action happens. English has three main tenses.

Present Tense – Describes actions happening now or actions that occur regularly.

Examples:

• She teaches Mathematics.

• Birds fly in the sky.

Past Tense – Describes actions that have already happened.

Examples:

• We visited the museum yesterday.

• The baby slept peacefully.

Future Tense – Describes actions that will happen later.

Examples:

• I will attend the conference next week.

• They shall arrive early tomorrow morning.

Common Errors in the Use of Verbs

Incorrect: She go to school every day.

Correct: She goes to school every day.

Incorrect: They was happy.

Correct: They were happy.

Incorrect: He have completed the assignment.

Correct: He has completed the assignment.

Importance of Verbs

• Verbs make complete sentences possible.

• They show actions, events and conditions.

• They help express time through different tenses.

• They improve both spoken and written communication.

• They are essential for correct grammar and sentence construction.

Exam Tips

⭐ Learn the different types of verbs and when to use them.

⭐ Understand the three main tenses and their forms.

⭐ Make sure verbs agree with their subjects.

⭐ Practise changing verbs from present to past and future tenses.

⭐ Read and write complete sentences using different verb forms correctly.

📘 CHAPTER 4: ADJECTIVE

An adjective is a word that describes or modifies a noun or pronoun. It provides more information about the size, shape, colour, age, quantity, quality, origin or condition of a person, place, animal or thing. Adjectives make sentences more interesting, meaningful and descriptive by helping readers and listeners understand exactly what is being referred to.

Without adjectives, communication would be less specific. For example, saying "a car" gives little information, but saying "a fast red sports car" creates a much clearer picture.

Examples of Adjectives

• beautiful

• tall

• blue

• intelligent

• expensive

• happy

• young

• large

Examples in Sentences

• She bought a beautiful dress.

• The tall building can be seen from far away.

• We adopted a small puppy.

• He solved the difficult problem quickly.

• They travelled in a comfortable bus.

Types of Adjectives

Descriptive Adjectives – These describe the quality or characteristics of a noun.

Examples: beautiful, brave, honest, strong, intelligent.

Quantitative Adjectives – These indicate the quantity or amount of something.

Examples: much, little, enough, sufficient.

Numeral Adjectives – These show number or order.

Examples: one, two, first, second, third.

Demonstrative Adjectives – These point out specific nouns.

Examples: this, that, these, those.

Possessive Adjectives – These show ownership or possession.

Examples: my, your, his, her, its, our, their.

Interrogative Adjectives – These are used with nouns to ask questions.

Examples: which, what, whose.

Degrees of Comparison

Many adjectives can be used to compare people, places or things. There are three degrees of comparison.

Positive Degree – Describes one person or thing without making a comparison.

Examples: tall, small, beautiful.

Comparative Degree – Compares two people or things.

Examples: taller, smaller, more beautiful.

Superlative Degree – Compares three or more people or things and identifies the highest or lowest degree.

Examples: tallest, smallest, most beautiful.

Examples in Sentences

• Mount Fuji is higher than many mountains in Japan.

• This is the largest library in the city.

• Her presentation was the most interesting of all.

Common Errors in the Use of Adjectives

Incorrect: She is the most tallest student in the class.

Correct: She is the tallest student in the class.

Incorrect: This book is more easier to understand.

Correct: This book is easier to understand.

Incorrect: He has a red beautiful car.

Correct: He has a beautiful red car.

Importance of Adjectives

• They make speech and writing more descriptive.

• They help identify and describe people, places and things accurately.

• They improve storytelling and creative writing.

• They make communication more precise and engaging.

• They help readers and listeners create clear mental images.

Exam Tips

⭐ Learn the different types of adjectives and their functions.

⭐ Understand the positive, comparative and superlative forms of adjectives.

⭐ Avoid using double comparatives and double superlatives.

⭐ Practise identifying adjectives in reading passages.

⭐ Use adjectives appropriately to improve sentence quality and clarity.

📘 CHAPTER 5: ADVERB

An adverb is a word that modifies or describes a verb, an adjective, another adverb or, in some cases, an entire sentence. Adverbs provide additional information about how, when, where, how often or to what extent an action happens. They make communication more precise by giving readers and listeners a clearer understanding of events and actions.

Many adverbs are formed by adding -ly to an adjective, although not all words ending in -ly are adverbs, and not all adverbs end in -ly.

Examples of Adverbs

• quickly

• carefully

• slowly

• always

• often

• yesterday

• outside

• very

Examples in Sentences

• She answered the question correctly.

• The athletes trained hard before the competition.

• We will leave tomorrow.

• He speaks very confidently during presentations.

• The children waited outside the building.

Types of Adverbs

Adverbs of Manner – These describe how an action is performed.

Examples: carefully, slowly, happily, quietly, confidently.

Examples in Sentences:

• The nurse treated the patient carefully.

• She completed the assignment quickly.

Adverbs of Time – These tell when an action happens.

Examples: today, yesterday, tomorrow, recently, soon.

Examples in Sentences:

• We arrived early.

• They will travel tomorrow.

Adverbs of Place – These indicate where an action happens.

Examples: here, there, outside, upstairs, everywhere.

Examples in Sentences:

• Please wait here.

• The children are playing outside.

Adverbs of Frequency – These show how often an action occurs.

Examples: always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never.

Examples in Sentences:

• She always keeps her promises.

• We sometimes visit the museum during holidays.

Adverbs of Degree – These describe the intensity or extent of an action, adjective or another adverb.

Examples: very, quite, almost, too, enough, extremely.

Examples in Sentences:

• The movie was extremely interesting.

• He was almost late for the meeting.

Position of Adverbs in a Sentence

The position of an adverb depends on its type and the meaning intended.

• Adverbs of manner usually come after the main verb.

Example: She sang beautifully.

• Adverbs of frequency usually come before the main verb but after the verb to be.

Examples:

• They often travel by train.

• She is always cheerful.

• Adverbs of time often appear at the beginning or end of a sentence.

Example: We will begin the lesson tomorrow.

Common Errors in the Use of Adverbs

Incorrect: She sings beautiful.

Correct: She sings beautifully.

Incorrect: He drives very careful.

Correct: He drives very carefully.

Incorrect: They always are late.

Correct: They are always late.

Importance of Adverbs

• They explain how, when, where and how often actions happen.

• They make speech and writing clearer and more detailed.

• They improve sentence variety and fluency.

• They help writers and speakers express ideas more accurately.

• They strengthen descriptive and creative writing.

Exam Tips

⭐ Identify the word being modified before choosing an adverb.

⭐ Learn the different types of adverbs and their functions.

⭐ Avoid confusing adjectives with adverbs.

⭐ Pay attention to the correct position of adverbs in sentences.

⭐ Practise identifying adverbs in reading passages and everyday conversations.

📘 CHAPTER 6: PREPOSITION

A preposition is a word that shows the relationship between a noun or pronoun and another word in a sentence. It often indicates direction, place, position, time, movement, cause, manner or possession. Prepositions help readers and listeners understand how people, objects and ideas are connected.

A preposition is usually followed by a noun or pronoun, forming a group of words known as a prepositional phrase.

Examples of Prepositions

• in

• on

• at

• under

• above

• between

• beside

• behind

• before

• after

• through

• across

• during

• without

• with

Examples in Sentences

• The keys are on the table.

• The children are playing in the garden.

• We arrived at the airport early.

• The cat is sleeping under the chair.

• She walked across the bridge.

Types of Prepositions

Prepositions of Place – These indicate the position or location of a person or object.

Examples: in, on, under, beside, behind, between, above, below.

Examples in Sentences:

• The books are on the shelf.

• The bicycle is behind the house.

Prepositions of Time – These show when something happens.

Examples: at, on, in, before, after, during, since, until.

Examples in Sentences:

• The meeting starts at 9:00 a.m.

• We travelled during the holidays.

Prepositions of Direction or Movement – These describe movement from one place to another.

Examples: into, onto, across, through, towards, along, over.

Examples in Sentences:

• The bird flew over the river.

• They walked through the forest.

Prepositions of Cause or Purpose – These explain the reason or purpose for an action.

Examples: because of, due to, for.

Examples in Sentences:

• The match was cancelled because of heavy rain.

• She thanked everyone for their support.

Common Prepositional Phrases

• in front of

• next to

• in addition to

• because of

• in spite of

• according to

• instead of

• by means of

Common Errors in the Use of Prepositions

Incorrect: She arrived to the airport early.

Correct: She arrived at the airport early.

Incorrect: We discussed about the project.

Correct: We discussed the project.

Incorrect: He is married with a doctor.

Correct: He is married to a doctor.

Incorrect: The meeting starts in Monday.

Correct: The meeting starts on Monday.

Importance of Prepositions

• They show relationships between words in a sentence.

• They make communication clear and meaningful.

• They help describe location, time, movement and direction accurately.

• They improve sentence structure and grammatical accuracy.

• They are essential for both spoken and written English.

Exam Tips

⭐ Learn the correct prepositions used with common verbs and expressions.

⭐ Pay attention to prepositions of time, place and movement.

⭐ Avoid translating directly from another language, as prepositions vary between languages.

⭐ Read English books regularly to become familiar with natural preposition usage.

⭐ Practise completing sentences by choosing the most appropriate preposition.

📘 CHAPTER 7: CONJUNCTION

A conjunction is a word that joins words, phrases or clauses together. Conjunctions help connect ideas, making sentences smoother, clearer and more meaningful. Without conjunctions, writing and speech would consist of many short, disconnected sentences.

Conjunctions are essential for expressing relationships such as addition, contrast, choice, reason, condition and time. They improve the flow of language and help organize ideas logically.

Examples of Conjunctions

• and

• but

• or

• because

• although

• since

• while

• unless

• if

• when

• before

• after

Examples in Sentences

• Emma and Noah completed the project together.

• The weather was cold, but we continued hiking.

• Would you like tea or coffee?

• The match was postponed because it was raining.

• Although he was tired, he finished his work.

Types of Conjunctions

Coordinating Conjunctions – These join words, phrases or independent clauses that are of equal importance.

Common Coordinating Conjunctions: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so (FANBOYS).

Examples in Sentences:

• Sofia enjoys reading and writing.

• We wanted to leave early, but the meeting continued.

• You can stay here or come with us.

Subordinating Conjunctions – These join a dependent clause to an independent clause.

Examples: because, although, since, while, if, unless, before, after, when, until.

Examples in Sentences:

• We stayed indoors because the storm was severe.

• If you study consistently, you will improve.

• She smiled when she received the news.

Correlative Conjunctions – These work in pairs to connect similar words or groups of words.

Examples: either...or, neither...nor, both...and, not only...but also, whether...or.

Examples in Sentences:

• Both the teacher and the students attended the workshop.

• Either we leave now or we miss the train.

• She is not only intelligent but also hardworking.

Functions of Conjunctions

• Join words of the same type.

• Connect phrases within a sentence.

• Link independent and dependent clauses.

• Express relationships such as contrast, reason, choice, condition and sequence.

• Improve the flow and clarity of writing and speech.

Common Errors in the Use of Conjunctions

Incorrect: Although he was tired but he continued working.

Correct: Although he was tired, he continued working.

Incorrect: Because it was late so we went home.

Correct: Because it was late, we went home.

Incorrect: Either Sarah or her brothers is coming.

Correct: Either Sarah or her brothers are coming.

Importance of Conjunctions

• They connect ideas smoothly.

• They help create longer and more meaningful sentences.

• They improve the logical flow of speech and writing.

• They reduce unnecessary repetition.

• They make communication more effective and easier to understand.

Exam Tips

⭐ Learn the three main types of conjunctions and their functions.

⭐ Memorize the coordinating conjunctions (FANBOYS).

⭐ Avoid using two conjunctions unnecessarily in the same sentence.

⭐ Practise joining short sentences using suitable conjunctions.

⭐ Read passages carefully to identify how conjunctions connect ideas.

📘 CHAPTER 8: INTERJECTION

An interjection is a word or short expression used to express sudden emotions, feelings or reactions. Unlike other parts of speech, an interjection is not grammatically connected to the rest of the sentence. Instead, it stands alone to show how the speaker feels at a particular moment.

People use interjections naturally in everyday conversations. For example, when someone receives exciting news, they may say "Hurray!" to express happiness. If a person accidentally touches something hot, they may immediately say "Ouch!" to express pain. Likewise, someone who sees something surprising may say "Wow!" or "Oh!" to show surprise.

Interjections make conversations more lively, natural and expressive because they help listeners understand the speaker's emotions. They are commonly followed by an exclamation mark (!) to show strong feelings, although a comma may be used when the emotion is less intense.

Examples of Interjections

• Wow!

• Oh!

• Ouch!

• Hurray!

• Alas!

• Bravo!

• Oops!

• Ah!

• Congratulations!

• Excellent!

Functions of Interjections

Expressing Joy – Interjections are used to show happiness, excitement or celebration.

Example: Hurray! Our team won the championship.

Expressing Surprise – They are used when something unexpected happens.

Example: Wow! That waterfall is beautiful.

Expressing Pain – They show physical pain or discomfort.

Example: Ouch! I hurt my finger.

Expressing Sadness – They express sorrow, disappointment or regret.

Example: Alas! The old building was destroyed by the fire.

Expressing Approval or Praise – They are used to congratulate or appreciate someone's effort.

Example: Bravo! You delivered an excellent speech.

Expressing Shock or Fear – They show sudden fear, surprise or disbelief.

Example: Oh! I didn't know you were here.

Using Interjections Correctly

Interjections are usually placed at the beginning of a sentence because they express the speaker's immediate reaction. They should be followed by an exclamation mark when expressing strong emotions. However, when the feeling is not very strong, a comma may be used instead.

Examples:

• Wow! That is an incredible painting.

• Oh, I almost forgot my keys.

• Hurray! We finally completed the project.

Common Errors in the Use of Interjections

Incorrect: Wow the view is amazing.

Correct: Wow! The view is amazing.

Incorrect: Alas We lost the competition.

Correct: Alas! We lost the competition.

Incorrect: Ouch I stepped on a sharp object.

Correct: Ouch! I stepped on a sharp object.

Importance of Interjections

• They help speakers express emotions quickly and naturally.

• They make conversations more lively and interesting.

• They help listeners understand the speaker's feelings.

• They make stories, speeches and dialogues more realistic.

• They improve both spoken and written communication.

Exam Tips

⭐ Know the meaning and functions of interjections.

⭐ Learn the common interjections and the emotions they express.

⭐ Use the correct punctuation after an interjection.

⭐ Avoid using too many interjections in formal writing.

⭐ Practise identifying interjections in conversations and reading passages.

📘 CHAPTER 9: ARTICLE

Articles are words placed before nouns to indicate whether the noun refers to something specific or general. Although articles are small words, they play a very important role in English because they help make the meaning of sentences clear and accurate.

English has three articles: a, an and the.

The articles a and an are called indefinite articles because they refer to a person, place, animal or thing in a general way. The article the is called the definite article because it refers to a particular person, place, animal or thing that is already known or has been identified.

Types of Articles

Indefinite Articles (A and An) – These are used when referring to a noun for the first time or when the noun is not specific.

Article "A" is used before words that begin with a consonant sound.

Examples:

• a book

• a teacher

• a university

• a European country

Examples in Sentences

• She bought a bicycle yesterday.

• He is a doctor.

• We visited a museum during our trip.

Article "An" is used before words that begin with a vowel sound.

Examples:

• an apple

• an elephant

• an umbrella

• an honest person

Examples in Sentences

• She ate an orange.

• He is an engineer.

• We met an honest police officer.

The Definite Article (The)

The article the is used when referring to a particular person, place, animal or thing that both the speaker and listener know about.

Examples in Sentences

• Please close the window.

• We visited the National Museum yesterday.

• The Earth revolves around the Sun.

• She returned the book that she borrowed.

When to Use Articles

Use "a" before singular countable nouns beginning with a consonant sound.

Example: I saw a bird.

Use "an" before singular countable nouns beginning with a vowel sound.

Example: She bought an umbrella.

Use "the" when talking about something specific or already mentioned.

Example: I bought a phone. The phone is very fast.

When Articles Are Not Used

Articles are generally not used before:

• Most proper nouns.

Example: Maria lives in Brazil.

• Names of most languages.

Example: She speaks English and Spanish.

• Most uncountable nouns when speaking generally.

Example: Water is essential for life.

• Plural nouns used in a general sense.

Example: Books help people learn.

Common Errors in the Use of Articles

Incorrect: He is a honest man.

Correct: He is an honest man.

Incorrect: She bought an university textbook.

Correct: She bought a university textbook.

Incorrect: Earth revolves around Sun.

Correct: The Earth revolves around the Sun.

Importance of Articles

• They help make sentences clear and meaningful.

• They distinguish between specific and general nouns.

• They improve grammatical accuracy.

• They make both spoken and written English more natural.

• They help learners communicate ideas correctly.

Exam Tips

⭐ Remember that a and an depend on pronunciation, not simply spelling.

⭐ Use the when referring to something specific or previously mentioned.

⭐ Do not use articles unnecessarily before proper nouns or plural nouns used generally.

⭐ Practise identifying where articles are required and where they should be omitted.

⭐ Read English texts regularly to become familiar with natural article usage.

📘 CHAPTER 10: SENTENCE STRUCTURE

Meaning of Sentence Structure

Sentence structure refers to the way words are arranged in a sentence to express a complete and meaningful idea. It explains how different parts of a sentence such as the subject, verb, object, phrases and clauses work together to communicate information clearly and correctly. A good understanding of sentence structure enables learners to speak and write English accurately, confidently and effectively.

Every language follows certain rules for arranging words, and English is no exception. If these rules are ignored, the message may become confusing or meaningless. Therefore, understanding sentence structure is one of the most important foundations of English grammar.

A well-structured sentence allows the reader or listener to understand the speaker's message without confusion. Whether writing an essay, a letter, an article or participating in a conversation, using correct sentence structure improves communication and helps ideas flow naturally.

Meaning of a Sentence

A sentence is a group of words arranged in the correct grammatical order to express a complete thought. Every complete sentence must contain at least a subject and a verb, and it must make complete sense on its own.

Examples:

• The children are playing in the park.

• My brother enjoys reading science books.

• The train arrived on time.

These sentences are complete because each contains a subject, a verb and a complete idea.

Characteristics of a Good Sentence

A good sentence should have the following qualities:

It must have a subject. The subject tells who or what the sentence is about.

It must have a verb. The verb tells the action performed or the state of being.

It must express a complete thought. The reader should clearly understand the intended message.

It must follow the correct grammatical order. Words should be arranged according to the rules of English grammar.

It should be clear and meaningful. Every sentence should communicate an idea without causing confusion.

Exam Tips

⭐ Understand the meaning of sentence structure and why it is important.

⭐ Learn the essential parts of a sentence, especially the subject and predicate.

⭐ Be able to identify simple, compound, complex and compound-complex sentences.

⭐ Practise correcting sentence fragments and run-on sentences.

⭐ Read English passages regularly to improve sentence construction.

⭐ Write complete sentences and check that every sentence contains a subject, a verb and a complete thought.

📘 CHAPTER 11: SUBJECT–VERB AGREEMENT

Meaning of Subject–Verb Agreement

Subject–verb agreement is one of the most important rules of English grammar. It means that the verb in a sentence must agree with its subject in both number and person. In other words, a singular subject takes a singular verb, while a plural subject takes a plural verb.

Using the correct verb with the correct subject makes sentences grammatically correct and easy to understand. When the subject and verb do not agree, the sentence becomes incorrect and may confuse the reader or listener. Therefore, understanding subject–verb agreement is essential for speaking and writing correct English.

Although the rule appears simple, learners often make mistakes because some subjects are misleading. Collective nouns, indefinite pronouns and compound subjects require special attention before choosing the correct verb. Learning these rules will help you avoid common grammatical errors.

Basic Rule of Subject–Verb Agreement

The basic rule of subject–verb agreement is simple:

A singular subject takes a singular verb.

A plural subject takes a plural verb.

Examples:

• The student reads every evening.

• The students read every evening.

• My brother plays football every weekend.

• My brothers play football every weekend.

Rules of Subject–Verb Agreement

Rule 1: A singular subject takes a singular verb.

Example: The doctor works in the hospital.

Rule 2: A plural subject takes a plural verb.

Example: The doctors work in the hospital.

Rule 3: Two or more subjects joined by and usually take a plural verb.

Example: John and Mary are classmates.

Rule 4: Subjects joined by either...or and neither...nor take the verb that agrees with the subject nearest to it.

Examples:

• Either the teacher or the students are responsible.

• Either the students or the teacher is responsible.

Importance of Subject–Verb Agreement

• It helps learners construct grammatically correct sentences.

• It improves both spoken and written English.

• It prevents confusion and misunderstanding.

• It makes communication more effective.

• It improves performance in English examinations.

Common Errors in Subject–Verb Agreement

Incorrect: The boys plays football.

Correct: The boys play football.

Incorrect: She go to school every day.

Correct: She goes to school every day.

Incorrect: My friend and my sister is here.

Correct: My friend and my sister are here.

Exam Tips

⭐ Understand the basic rule that singular subjects take singular verbs, while plural subjects take plural verbs.

⭐ Learn how verbs agree with compound subjects joined by and, either...or and neither...nor.

⭐ Identify the subject carefully before choosing the correct verb.

⭐ Practise correcting sentences with subject–verb agreement errors.

⭐ Read English passages regularly to become familiar with correct sentence patterns.

📘 CHAPTER 12: TENSES

Meaning of Tenses

Tenses are forms of verbs that show the time at which an action takes place. They tell us whether an action happened in the past, is happening in the present or will happen in the future. Tenses are one of the most important aspects of English grammar because they help speakers and writers communicate ideas accurately and clearly.

Without the correct use of tenses, it would be difficult to know when an event occurred. For example, the sentences "She writes a letter," "She wrote a letter," and "She will write a letter" all describe the same action, but each one refers to a different time. Therefore, understanding tenses is essential for effective communication.

English has twelve main tenses. These tenses are grouped into three major categories: the Present Tense, the Past Tense and the Future Tense. Each category has four forms: Simple, Continuous, Perfect and Perfect Continuous.

The Three Main Tenses

Present Tense – This tense describes actions that happen now, actions that happen regularly or facts that are always true.

Examples:

• The Earth revolves around the Sun.

• She studies every evening.

• They are playing football.

Past Tense – This tense describes actions that happened and were completed in the past.

Examples:

• We visited the museum yesterday.

• She completed her assignment last night.

• They were watching television when I arrived.

Future Tense – This tense describes actions that will happen in the future.

Examples:

• We will travel next month.

• She will complete the project tomorrow.

• They will be waiting for us at the airport.

The Twelve English Tenses

1. Simple Present Tense

Used for habits, routines, general truths and repeated actions.

Example: She teaches Mathematics.

2. Present Continuous Tense

Used for actions happening at the moment of speaking.

Example: She is teaching Mathematics.

3. Present Perfect Tense

Used for actions completed recently or actions that still affect the present.

Example: She has finished her work.

4. Present Perfect Continuous Tense

Used for actions that began in the past and are still continuing.

Example: She has been reading for two hours.

5. Simple Past Tense

Used for completed actions in the past.

Example: They travelled to Canada last year.

6. Past Continuous Tense

Used for actions that were in progress at a particular time in the past.

Example: They were travelling when the storm began.

7. Past Perfect Tense

Used for an action completed before another action in the past.

Example: She had left before the meeting started.

8. Past Perfect Continuous Tense

Used for an action that continued for some time before another past action.

Example: He had been working for five hours before he rested.

9. Simple Future Tense

Used for actions that will happen in the future.

Example: We will attend the conference next week.

10. Future Continuous Tense

Used for actions that will be in progress at a particular time in the future.

Example: This time tomorrow, we will be travelling.

11. Future Perfect Tense

Used for actions that will have been completed before a certain future time.

Example: She will have completed the course by July.

12. Future Perfect Continuous Tense

Used for actions that will have continued for a period of time before another future event.

Example: By next month, they will have been living here for five years.

Importance of Tenses

• They show the time an action takes place.

• They improve accuracy in speaking and writing.

• They help readers and listeners understand events clearly.

• They make communication more effective.

• They are essential for academic and professional writing.

Common Errors in the Use of Tenses

Incorrect: She go to school every day.

Correct: She goes to school every day.

Incorrect: They has completed the assignment.

Correct: They have completed the assignment.

Incorrect: I am knowing the answer.

Correct: I know the answer.

Exam Tips

⭐ Understand the difference between the Present, Past and Future Tenses.

⭐ Learn the twelve English tenses and when each one is used.

⭐ Pay attention to time expressions such as yesterday, now, tomorrow, since, for, already and yet.

⭐ Practise changing sentences from one tense to another.

⭐ Read English passages regularly to observe how different tenses are used naturally.

📘 CHAPTER 13: ACTIVE AND PASSIVE VOICE

Meaning of Active and Passive Voice

Active and Passive Voice are two different ways of expressing the same idea in English. They describe the relationship between the subject, the verb and the object in a sentence. Understanding the difference between the two voices helps learners improve their writing, speaking and overall communication skills.

In the Active Voice, the subject performs the action expressed by the verb. In the Passive Voice, the subject receives the action performed by someone or something else. Although both forms express the same idea, they place emphasis on different parts of the sentence.

The Active Voice is more common in everyday communication because it is direct, clear and easy to understand. The Passive Voice is often used in scientific writing, formal reports, news reports and situations where the performer of the action is unknown or less important than the action itself.

Active Voice

A sentence is in the Active Voice when the subject performs the action of the verb. This makes the sentence straightforward, lively and easy to understand.

Structure:

Subject + Verb + Object

Examples:

• The teacher explained the lesson.

• Sarah wrote the report.

• The engineer designed the bridge.

• The children cleaned the classroom.

Passive Voice

A sentence is in the Passive Voice when the subject receives the action of the verb. The object of the active sentence becomes the subject of the passive sentence.

Structure:

Object + Form of "be" + Past Participle + (by + Subject)

Examples:

• The lesson was explained by the teacher.

• The report was written by Sarah.

• The bridge was designed by the engineer.

• The classroom was cleaned by the children.

Differences Between Active and Passive Voice

Active Voice emphasizes the person or thing performing the action.

Passive Voice emphasizes the person or thing receiving the action.

Active Voice is usually shorter and clearer.

Passive Voice is often longer because it requires an additional helping verb.

Examples:

Active: The scientist discovered a new medicine.

Passive: A new medicine was discovered by the scientist.

When to Use the Passive Voice

• When the person performing the action is unknown.

Example: The window was broken last night.

• When the action is more important than the person performing it.

Example: A new airport has been built.

• In scientific, technical and formal writing.

Example: The experiment was conducted under controlled conditions.

How to Change Active Voice to Passive Voice

1. Identify the subject, verb and object.

2. Make the object become the subject of the new sentence.

3. Use the correct form of the verb "be".

4. Change the main verb into its past participle form.

5. Add "by" followed by the original subject when necessary.

Example:

Active: The manager approved the proposal.

Passive: The proposal was approved by the manager.

Importance of Active and Passive Voice

• Improves writing and speaking skills.

• Helps learners write formal reports and academic papers.

• Makes communication more flexible and effective.

• Helps writers emphasize important information.

• Improves grammatical accuracy.

Common Errors in Active and Passive Voice

Incorrect: The letter wrote by John.

Correct: The letter was written by John.

Incorrect: The homework was completed John.

Correct: The homework was completed by John.

Incorrect: The cake was ate by the children.

Correct: The cake was eaten by the children.

Exam Tips

⭐ Learn the difference between Active Voice and Passive Voice.

⭐ Practise changing sentences from Active Voice to Passive Voice and vice versa.

⭐ Remember that only transitive verbs can normally be changed into the Passive Voice.

⭐ Master the past participle forms of common irregular verbs.

⭐ Identify the subject, verb and object before attempting any transformation.

📘 CHAPTER 14: DIRECT AND INDIRECT SPEECH

Meaning of Direct and Indirect Speech

Direct and Indirect Speech are two ways of reporting what another person has said. They are important aspects of English grammar because they enable speakers and writers to communicate conversations, instructions, questions and statements accurately. Understanding how to change Direct Speech into Indirect Speech helps learners improve their speaking, writing and comprehension skills.

In everyday communication, people often repeat what others have said. This can be done by quoting the exact words spoken or by reporting the message in a different form while keeping the original meaning. These two methods are known as Direct Speech and Indirect Speech.

Direct Speech gives the speaker's exact words, while Indirect Speech reports the speaker's message without repeating the exact words. Both forms are widely used in conversations, newspapers, stories, academic writing and official reports.

Direct Speech

Direct Speech, also known as quoted speech, presents the exact words spoken by a person. The spoken words are placed inside quotation marks (" ") and are usually introduced by a reporting verb such as said, asked, replied, answered or told.

Examples:

• Sarah said, "I am preparing for my examination."

• The teacher said, "Complete your assignment before tomorrow."

• David asked, "Where is the library?"

Indirect Speech

Indirect Speech, also known as reported speech, reports the meaning of what someone said without using the exact words. Quotation marks are not used, and certain words such as pronouns, verbs, time expressions and place expressions may change depending on the situation.

Examples:

• Sarah said that she was preparing for her examination.

• The teacher told the students to complete their assignment before the following day.

• David asked where the library was.

Differences Between Direct and Indirect Speech

Direct Speech uses the speaker's exact words.

Indirect Speech reports the meaning of the speaker's words.

Direct Speech uses quotation marks.

Indirect Speech does not use quotation marks.

Direct Speech usually begins with a comma before the quotation.

Indirect Speech often begins with the word that, although it may sometimes be omitted.

Rules for Changing Direct Speech to Indirect Speech

Rule 1: Remove the quotation marks.

Rule 2: Change the pronouns where necessary.

Rule 3: Change the tense of the verb when required.

Rule 4: Change words expressing time and place where appropriate.

Examples:

• today → that day

• tomorrow → the next day

• yesterday → the previous day

• here → there

• now → then

Importance of Direct and Indirect Speech

• Helps learners report conversations correctly.

• Improves speaking and writing skills.

• Makes storytelling and reporting more effective.

• Enhances communication in academic and professional settings.

• Improves grammatical accuracy.

Common Errors in Direct and Indirect Speech

Incorrect: John said that "I am tired."

Correct: John said that he was tired.

Incorrect: She said, I am happy.

Correct: She said, "I am happy."

Incorrect: He said he is coming yesterday.

Correct: He said that he was coming the previous day.

Exam Tips

⭐ Know the difference between Direct Speech and Indirect Speech.

⭐ Learn the rules for changing pronouns, tenses and time expressions.

⭐ Use quotation marks correctly in Direct Speech.

⭐ Practise converting Direct Speech into Indirect Speech and vice versa.

⭐ Read stories and dialogues regularly to observe how reported speech is used naturally.

📘 CHAPTER 15: PUNCTUATION AND CAPITALIZATION

Meaning of Punctuation and Capitalization

Punctuation and capitalization are essential features of written English that help make communication clear, accurate and meaningful. Punctuation consists of the symbols used in writing to separate ideas, indicate pauses, show emphasis and organize sentences correctly. Capitalization refers to the use of capital letters at the beginning of sentences and for specific words according to the rules of English grammar.

Without proper punctuation and capitalization, written communication can become confusing or difficult to understand. Correct use of these rules enables readers to interpret ideas accurately and improves the overall quality of writing. Whether writing essays, letters, reports, articles or examination answers, learners should always pay attention to punctuation and capitalization.

Meaning of Punctuation

Punctuation is the system of using symbols in writing to clarify meaning and indicate how a sentence should be read. Each punctuation mark has a specific purpose and contributes to making written English easier to understand.

Common Punctuation Marks

Full Stop (.) – Used at the end of a statement.

Example: The meeting ended at noon.

Question Mark (?) – Used at the end of a direct question.

Example: Where do you live?

Exclamation Mark (!) – Used to express strong emotions, surprise or excitement.

Example: What a beautiful view!

Comma (,) – Used to separate items in a list, introductory words and clauses.

Example: We bought apples, oranges, bananas and grapes.

Colon (:) – Used before introducing a list, explanation or quotation.

Example: Bring the following items: a notebook, a pen and a calculator.

Semicolon (;) – Used to join closely related independent clauses.

Example: The weather was cold; however, we continued the journey.

Quotation Marks (" ") – Used to show the exact words spoken by someone.

Example: She said, "Practice makes perfect."

Apostrophe (') – Used to show possession or contraction.

Examples:

• Maria's book.

• They're going home.

Meaning of Capitalization

Capitalization is the use of capital letters according to the rules of English grammar. Capital letters help identify the beginning of sentences, proper nouns and other important words.

Rules of Capitalization

Rule 1: Begin every sentence with a capital letter.

Example: Learning English requires regular practice.

Rule 2: Capitalize proper nouns.

Examples: Canada, Africa, Maria, London, Pacific Ocean.

Rule 3: Capitalize the days of the week, months and public holidays.

Examples: Monday, August, Christmas.

Rule 4: Capitalize the pronoun I wherever it appears.

Example: My friend and I attended the seminar.

Rule 5: Capitalize the first word in a quotation.

Example: The teacher said, "Always do your best."

Importance of Punctuation and Capitalization

• They make writing clear and easy to understand.

• They prevent misunderstanding and ambiguity.

• They improve grammar and writing accuracy.

• They make formal writing more professional.

• They help readers understand ideas correctly.

Common Errors in Punctuation and Capitalization

Incorrect: where are you going.

Correct: Where are you going?

Incorrect: i visited paris last july.

Correct: I visited Paris last July.

Incorrect: she said "good morning".

Correct: She said, "Good morning."

Exam Tips

⭐ Learn the functions of the common punctuation marks.

⭐ Always begin a sentence with a capital letter.

⭐ Capitalize proper nouns, days, months and the pronoun I.

⭐ Use punctuation marks correctly in essays, letters and comprehension passages.

⭐ Proofread your work before submitting it to correct punctuation and capitalization errors.

📘 CHAPTER 16: LEXIS AND STRUCTURE

Meaning of Lexis and Structure

Lexis and Structure is an important aspect of English Language that deals with vocabulary and grammar. Lexis refers to the words and expressions used in a language, while Structure refers to the grammatical rules that govern how words are arranged to form meaningful sentences. Together, they help people communicate ideas clearly, accurately and effectively.

A good knowledge of Lexis and Structure enables learners to choose the right words and arrange them correctly in sentences. This is important in both spoken and written English because incorrect word choice or poor sentence structure can change the intended meaning of a message.

In many English examinations, Lexis and Structure tests a learner's ability to use vocabulary correctly, recognize grammatical errors and apply the rules of English in different contexts. Therefore, mastering this topic is essential for academic success and effective communication.

Meaning of Lexis

Lexis refers to the vocabulary of a language. It includes individual words, phrases, expressions and idioms used in communication. A rich vocabulary enables a person to express ideas more accurately and confidently.

Examples of Lexical Items:

• Happy

• Beautiful

• Opportunity

• Environment

• Make a decision

• Break the ice

Meaning of Structure

Structure refers to the grammatical arrangement of words in a sentence. It explains how nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs and other parts of speech work together to produce meaningful and grammatically correct sentences.

Examples:

• She speaks English fluently.

• The students completed the assignment successfully.

• They will arrive tomorrow morning.

Components of Lexis and Structure

Vocabulary – Choosing the correct words according to their meanings.

Grammar – Applying grammatical rules correctly.

Sentence Construction – Arranging words to express complete ideas.

Word Forms – Using nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs appropriately.

Idiomatic Expressions – Understanding expressions whose meanings cannot be interpreted literally.

Importance of Lexis and Structure

• Improves vocabulary development.

• Enhances grammar and sentence construction.

• Promotes effective spoken and written communication.

• Improves reading comprehension.

• Helps learners perform better in English examinations.

Common Errors in Lexis and Structure

Correct: She enjoys reading books.

Correct: I look forward to seeing you.

Correct: The information is useful.

• Study English grammar consistently.

Exam Tips

📘 CHAPTER 17: COMPREHENSION

Meaning of Comprehension

Comprehension is the ability to read, understand, interpret and explain written or spoken information correctly. It involves more than simply recognizing words on a page; it requires the reader to understand the meaning of the text, identify important ideas and draw logical conclusions from what has been read.

Reading comprehension is an essential language skill because it enables learners to gain knowledge, solve problems and communicate effectively. A learner with good comprehension skills can identify the writer's purpose, understand the main ideas, recognize supporting details and answer questions accurately.

Comprehension plays an important role in education because almost every subject requires students to read and understand information. Strong comprehension skills also improve vocabulary, grammar, critical thinking and writing ability.

Purpose of Comprehension

The main purpose of comprehension is to help learners understand information correctly and use it effectively. It also develops analytical thinking and encourages readers to interpret ideas rather than merely memorize facts.

Types of Comprehension

Literal Comprehension – Understanding information that is directly stated in the passage.

Example: Identifying names, dates, places and facts mentioned in the text.

Inferential Comprehension – Understanding ideas that are implied rather than directly stated.

Example: Drawing conclusions based on clues in the passage.

Critical Comprehension – Evaluating the writer's ideas, opinions and arguments.

Example: Deciding whether the writer's opinion is convincing.

Creative Comprehension – Applying ideas from the passage to new situations or expressing personal opinions based on what has been read.

Skills Required for Good Comprehension

• Reading carefully and attentively.

• Identifying the main idea of a passage.

• Recognizing supporting details.

• Understanding unfamiliar words through context.

• Drawing logical conclusions.

• Summarizing important information.

• Answering questions accurately based on the passage.

How to Improve Comprehension Skills

• Read books, newspapers and articles regularly.

• Build your vocabulary by learning new words every day.

• Read each passage carefully before answering questions.

• Identify the topic, main idea and supporting details.

• Avoid making assumptions that are not supported by the passage.

• Practise answering comprehension questions frequently.

Importance of Comprehension

• Improves reading and learning ability.

• Expands vocabulary.

• Develops critical and analytical thinking.

• Improves writing and speaking skills.

• Helps learners perform better in examinations.

• Encourages independent learning.

Common Errors in Comprehension

Error 1: Reading too quickly without understanding the passage.

Solution: Read carefully and identify the main ideas before answering questions.

Error 2: Guessing answers without referring to the passage.

Solution: Base every answer on information provided in the text.

Error 3: Ignoring unfamiliar words.

Solution: Use context clues to determine their meanings.

Exam Tips

⭐ Read the passage completely before looking at the questions.

⭐ Identify the main idea and important supporting details.

⭐ Use context clues to understand unfamiliar words.

⭐ Base your answers on the information given in the passage.

⭐ Read each question carefully before selecting or writing your answer.

⭐ Manage your time wisely during comprehension exercises.

📘 CHAPTER 18: SUMMARY WRITING

Meaning of Summary Writing

Summary writing is the process of presenting the main ideas of a passage in a shorter and clearer form without changing the original meaning. A summary focuses only on the most important points while leaving out unnecessary details, examples, illustrations and repetitions.

The purpose of summary writing is to help readers understand the essential information in a passage quickly and accurately. A good summary is brief, well-organized and written in the writer's own words. It should faithfully represent the author's ideas without adding personal opinions or new information.

Summary writing is an important skill in education, business and everyday communication. Students use it to study textbooks, professionals use it to prepare reports, and researchers use it to present key findings from lengthy documents.

Purpose of Summary Writing

Summary writing helps readers identify the main ideas of a passage while ignoring less important information. It improves understanding, saves time and enables learners to remember important points more easily.

Characteristics of a Good Summary

It is Brief – A summary should be much shorter than the original passage.

It Contains the Main Ideas – Only the essential points should be included.

It Uses the Writer's Own Words – Avoid copying long sentences directly from the original passage.

It Maintains the Original Meaning – The ideas should remain accurate and complete.

It is Well Organized – Ideas should follow a logical order.

Steps in Writing a Good Summary

Step 1: Read the passage carefully until you understand it completely.

Step 2: Identify the main idea and supporting points.

Step 3: Ignore examples, illustrations and unnecessary details.

Step 4: Rewrite the important ideas in your own words.

Step 5: Arrange the ideas logically and check that the meaning has not changed.

Qualities of an Effective Summary

• It is clear and easy to understand.

• It is concise and free from unnecessary details.

• It is grammatically correct.

• It presents only relevant information.

• It accurately reflects the original passage.

Importance of Summary Writing

• Improves reading comprehension.

• Develops critical thinking skills.

• Improves writing ability.

• Helps learners identify important information.

• Saves time when studying long passages.

• Enhances communication and note-taking skills.

Common Errors in Summary Writing

Error 1: Copying the original passage word for word.

Solution: Rewrite the ideas using your own words.

Error 2: Including unnecessary examples and illustrations.

Solution: Focus only on the main ideas.

Error 3: Adding personal opinions.

Solution: Present only the author's ideas.

Error 4: Omitting important points.

Solution: Ensure every major idea is included.

Exam Tips

⭐ Read the passage at least twice before writing your summary.

⭐ Identify the topic sentence and the main supporting ideas.

⭐ Write in your own words as much as possible.

⭐ Avoid copying long expressions directly from the passage.

⭐ Keep your summary brief, accurate and well organized.

⭐ Proofread your work before submitting it.

📘 CHAPTER 19: ESSAY WRITING

Meaning of Essay Writing

Essay writing is the process of expressing ideas, opinions, facts or arguments on a particular topic in a clear, organized and logical manner. An essay is a piece of writing that allows the writer to explain, describe, narrate or discuss a subject in detail. It is one of the most important aspects of English Language because it develops communication, reasoning and creative thinking skills.

Essay writing is widely used in schools, universities, workplaces and everyday life. Students write essays to demonstrate their understanding of different subjects, while professionals use essays and reports to present information, explain ideas and persuade readers. A well-written essay communicates the writer's thoughts effectively and leaves a lasting impression on the reader.

Good essay writing requires proper planning, correct grammar, appropriate vocabulary and logical organization of ideas. Every essay should have a clear beginning, a well-developed body and a meaningful conclusion.

Parts of an Essay

Introduction – The introduction is the opening paragraph of an essay. It introduces the topic, captures the reader's attention and gives a brief idea of what the essay will discuss.

Body – The body contains the main discussion of the essay. It is usually divided into paragraphs, with each paragraph explaining one main idea supported by facts, examples or explanations.

Conclusion – The conclusion is the final part of the essay. It summarizes the main points and brings the discussion to a logical ending without introducing new ideas.

Types of Essays

Narrative Essay – A narrative essay tells a story or describes an event in the order in which it happened.

Examples:

• An Unforgettable Journey.

• My First Day at School.

Descriptive Essay – A descriptive essay gives a detailed description of a person, place, object or event.

Examples:

• My Favourite Teacher.

• A Beautiful Beach.

Expository Essay – An expository essay explains or provides information about a topic using facts and logical explanations.

Examples:

• The Importance of Education.

• Causes of Climate Change.

Argumentative Essay – An argumentative essay presents arguments for or against a particular issue with the aim of convincing the reader.

Examples:

• Should School Uniforms Be Compulsory?

• Is Technology More Helpful Than Harmful?

Qualities of a Good Essay

• A clear and interesting introduction.

• Well-organized paragraphs.

• Logical development of ideas.

• Correct grammar and punctuation.

• Appropriate vocabulary.

• A strong and meaningful conclusion.

Steps in Writing a Good Essay

Step 1: Understand the topic carefully.

Step 2: Brainstorm and organize your ideas.

Step 3: Prepare a simple outline before writing.

Step 4: Write an interesting introduction.

Step 5: Develop each idea in separate paragraphs.

Step 6: Write a conclusion that summarizes the discussion.

Step 7: Proofread your essay to correct grammatical and spelling errors.

Importance of Essay Writing

• Develops communication skills.

• Improves creativity and critical thinking.

• Enhances grammar and vocabulary.

• Improves organization of ideas.

• Prepares learners for academic and professional writing.

• Builds confidence in expressing opinions.

Common Errors in Essay Writing

Error 1: Writing without planning.

Solution: Prepare an outline before writing.

Error 2: Using poor paragraph organization.

Solution: Let each paragraph discuss one main idea.

Error 3: Using incorrect grammar and punctuation.

Solution: Proofread the essay carefully before submission.

Error 4: Repeating the same ideas unnecessarily.

Solution: Present new supporting points in each paragraph.

Exam Tips

⭐ Read the essay question carefully before you begin writing.

⭐ Prepare a brief outline before developing your essay.

⭐ Write a clear introduction, body and conclusion.

⭐ Support your ideas with relevant facts, examples and explanations.

⭐ Use correct grammar, punctuation and appropriate vocabulary.

⭐ Leave a few minutes to proofread your work before submitting it.

📘 CHAPTER 20: ORAL ENGLISH

Meaning of Oral English

Oral English is the branch of English Language that deals with the correct pronunciation of words, sounds, stress, rhythm and intonation during speech. It focuses on how English should be spoken so that communication is clear, accurate and easily understood by listeners.

Unlike written English, which emphasizes grammar and spelling, Oral English is concerned with the correct production of speech sounds and the natural flow of spoken language. Good pronunciation helps speakers express themselves confidently and reduces misunderstanding in everyday communication.

Oral English is widely used in classrooms, interviews, public speaking, broadcasting, business communication and international interactions. Developing good Oral English skills enables learners to communicate effectively with people from different parts of the world.

Components of Oral English

Pronunciation – Pronunciation refers to the correct way of producing the sounds of words.

Examples:

• Comfortable

• Vegetable

• Education

Stress – Stress is the extra force or emphasis placed on a particular syllable in a word or on a particular word in a sentence.

Examples:

• PREsent (noun)

• preSENT (verb)

Intonation – Intonation is the rise and fall of the voice while speaking. It helps express meaning, emotions and attitudes.

Examples:

• Rising intonation is commonly used in yes/no questions.

• Falling intonation is commonly used in statements and commands.

Rhythm – Rhythm refers to the pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables in spoken English, making speech sound natural and fluent.

Vowel Sounds

English contains vowel sounds that may be short or long. Correct pronunciation of vowel sounds helps distinguish between words with similar spellings but different meanings.

Examples:

• ship / sheep

• full / fool

• sit / seat

Consonant Sounds

Consonant sounds are produced when the airflow is partly or completely blocked by the lips, teeth or tongue. Correct pronunciation of consonants improves clarity in speech.

Examples:

• fan / van

• think / sink

• zip / sip

Importance of Oral English

• Improves pronunciation and fluency.

• Enhances confidence in speaking.

• Reduces communication errors.

• Improves listening and speaking skills.

• Prepares learners for interviews, presentations and public speaking.

• Promotes effective communication in international environments.

Common Errors in Oral English

Error 1: Mispronouncing words because of spelling.

Solution: Learn the correct pronunciation using dictionaries or pronunciation guides.

Error 2: Placing stress on the wrong syllable.

Solution: Practise word stress regularly.

Error 3: Speaking with incorrect intonation.

Solution: Listen to fluent English speakers and imitate natural speech patterns.

Error 4: Confusing similar vowel or consonant sounds.

Solution: Practise minimal pairs such as ship/sheep and fan/van.

How to Improve Oral English

• Listen to native and fluent English speakers.

• Read aloud every day.

• Practise difficult sounds regularly.

• Learn correct word stress and sentence intonation.

• Record your voice and compare it with standard pronunciation.

• Speak English confidently whenever possible.

Exam Tips

⭐ Learn the correct pronunciation of commonly tested words.

⭐ Practise vowel and consonant sounds regularly.

⭐ Understand word stress and sentence stress.

⭐ Learn the basic patterns of English intonation.

⭐ Listen carefully during Oral English practice and imitate accurate pronunciation.

⭐ Revise past examination questions on Oral English frequently.
