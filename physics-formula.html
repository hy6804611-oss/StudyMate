📂 Measurement and Units

Density

ρ = m / V

📖 Meaning: Density is the mass per unit volume of a substance.

🎯 When to Use: Used to calculate density, mass or volume.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Density = Mass ÷ Volume
  • Mass = Density × Volume
  • Volume = Mass ÷ Density

💡 SI Unit: kg/m³

⚠️ Common Mistake: Do not confuse density with weight.

Relative Density

Relative Density = Density of Substance / Density of Water

📖 Meaning: Relative density compares the density of a substance with water.

🎯 When to Use: Used to determine whether an object floats or sinks.

💡 Note: Relative Density has no unit.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Do not write kg/m³ as its unit.

Pressure

P = F / A

📖 Meaning: Pressure is the force acting per unit area.

🎯 When to Use: Used in solids and fluids.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Pressure = Force ÷ Area
  • Force = Pressure × Area
  • Area = Force ÷ Pressure

💡 SI Unit: Pascal (Pa)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Area must always be in square metres (m²).

Mass

m = ρV

📖 Meaning: Mass is obtained by multiplying density by volume.

🎯 When to Use: When density and volume are known.

💡 SI Unit: Kilogram (kg)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Ensure volume is in cubic metres (m³).

📂 Scalars and Vectors

Resultant Vector

R = A + B

📖 Meaning: The resultant vector is the single vector equivalent to two or more vectors acting together.

🎯 When to Use: Used when combining vectors acting in the same or different directions.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Resultant = Vector A + Vector B
  • A = R − B
  • B = R − A

💡 SI Unit: Same as the vector quantity (e.g. m, N, m/s).

⚠️ Common Mistake: Scalars are added normally, but vectors must consider direction.

Magnitude of a Vector

|A| = √(Aₓ² + Aᵧ²)

📖 Meaning: Gives the length or size of a vector from its horizontal and vertical components.

🎯 When to Use: Used when vector components are known.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • |A| = √(Aₓ² + Aᵧ²)
  • Aₓ = |A| cos θ
  • Aᵧ = |A| sin θ

💡 SI Unit: Same as the vector quantity.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Do not add components directly without using Pythagoras.

Horizontal Component

Aₓ = A cos θ

📖 Meaning: Calculates the horizontal component of a vector.

🎯 When to Use: Resolving a vector into horizontal and vertical parts.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Aₓ = A cos θ
  • A = Aₓ / cos θ
  • cos θ = Aₓ / A

💡 SI Unit: Same as the original vector.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Use cosine for the horizontal component.

Vertical Component

Aᵧ = A sin θ

📖 Meaning: Calculates the vertical component of a vector.

🎯 When to Use: Resolving vectors in projectile and force problems.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Aᵧ = A sin θ
  • A = Aᵧ / sin θ
  • sin θ = Aᵧ / A

💡 SI Unit: Same as the original vector.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Use sine for the vertical component, not cosine.

Direction of a Vector

θ = tan⁻¹(Aᵧ / Aₓ)

📖 Meaning: Finds the angle of a vector measured from the horizontal.

🎯 When to Use: When horizontal and vertical components are known.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • θ = tan⁻¹(Aᵧ / Aₓ)
  • tan θ = Aᵧ / Aₓ
  • Aᵧ = Aₓ tan θ

💡 SI Unit: Degrees (°) or Radians (rad).

⚠️ Common Mistake: Ensure your calculator is in the correct angle mode (Degrees or Radians).

📂 Motion (Kinematics)

Speed

Speed = Distance / Time

📖 Meaning: Speed is the distance travelled per unit time.

🎯 When to Use: Used to calculate speed, distance or time.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Speed = Distance ÷ Time
  • Distance = Speed × Time
  • Time = Distance ÷ Speed

💡 SI Unit: metre per second (m/s)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Convert km/h to m/s when required.

Velocity

Velocity = Displacement / Time

📖 Meaning: Velocity is the rate of change of displacement in a particular direction.

🎯 When to Use: Used when direction is important.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Velocity = Displacement ÷ Time
  • Displacement = Velocity × Time
  • Time = Displacement ÷ Velocity

💡 SI Unit: metre per second (m/s)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Do not confuse velocity with speed.

Acceleration

a = (v − u) / t

📖 Meaning: Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity.

🎯 When to Use: Used when initial velocity, final velocity and time are known.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Acceleration = (Final Velocity − Initial Velocity) ÷ Time
  • v = u + at
  • u = v − at
  • t = (v − u) ÷ a

💡 SI Unit: metre per second squared (m/s²)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Always subtract initial velocity from final velocity.

First Equation of Motion

v = u + at

📖 Meaning: Relates final velocity, initial velocity, acceleration and time.

🎯 When to Use: Motion with constant acceleration.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • v = u + at
  • u = v − at
  • a = (v − u) ÷ t
  • t = (v − u) ÷ a

💡 SI Unit: Velocity (m/s)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Use consistent SI units throughout.

Second Equation of Motion

s = ut + ½at²

📖 Meaning: Calculates displacement during uniformly accelerated motion.

🎯 When to Use: When time is known but final velocity is not.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • s = ut + ½at²
  • If u = 0, then s = ½at²

💡 SI Unit: metre (m)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Remember to square the time (t²).

Third Equation of Motion

v² = u² + 2as

📖 Meaning: Relates velocity, acceleration and displacement without using time.

🎯 When to Use: When time is not given.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • v² = u² + 2as
  • s = (v² − u²) / 2a
  • a = (v² − u²) / 2s

💡 SI Unit: Velocity (m/s), Distance (m)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Square the velocities before substituting values.

📂 Force and Newton's Laws

Newton's Second Law

F = ma

📖 Meaning: Force is the product of mass and acceleration.

🎯 When to Use: Used to calculate force, mass or acceleration.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Force = Mass × Acceleration
  • Mass = Force ÷ Acceleration
  • Acceleration = Force ÷ Mass

💡 SI Unit: Newton (N)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Mass must be in kilograms (kg), not grams.

Weight

W = mg

📖 Meaning: Weight is the gravitational force acting on an object.

🎯 When to Use: Used to calculate weight, mass or gravitational field strength.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Weight = Mass × g
  • Mass = Weight ÷ g
  • g = Weight ÷ Mass

💡 SI Unit: Newton (N)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Weight is a force (N), while mass is measured in kg.

Resultant Force

Fnet = ΣF

📖 Meaning: The resultant force is the vector sum of all forces acting on an object.

🎯 When to Use: Used to determine whether an object accelerates or remains in equilibrium.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Resultant Force = Sum of all Forces
  • If ΣF = 0 → Object is in equilibrium.

💡 SI Unit: Newton (N)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Always consider the direction of each force.

Frictional Force

F = μR

📖 Meaning: Friction equals the coefficient of friction multiplied by the normal reaction.

🎯 When to Use: Used in friction problems on rough surfaces.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Friction = μ × Reaction
  • μ = F ÷ R
  • Reaction = F ÷ μ

💡 SI Unit: Newton (N)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Use the normal reaction force, not the object's weight unless they are equal.

Impulse

J = Ft = Δp

📖 Meaning: Impulse equals the change in momentum and is the product of force and time.

🎯 When to Use: Used in collision and impact problems.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Impulse = Force × Time
  • Force = Impulse ÷ Time
  • Time = Impulse ÷ Force

💡 SI Unit: Newton-second (N·s)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Impulse is equal to change in momentum, not momentum itself.

📂 Work, Energy and Power

Work Done

W = Fd

📖 Meaning: Work is done when a force moves an object through a distance.

🎯 When to Use: Used to calculate work, force or distance.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Work = Force × Distance
  • Force = Work ÷ Distance
  • Distance = Work ÷ Force

💡 SI Unit: Joule (J)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Distance must be in the direction of the force.

Kinetic Energy

KE = ½mv²

📖 Meaning: Kinetic energy is the energy possessed by a moving object.

🎯 When to Use: Used when mass and velocity are known.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • KE = ½mv²
  • m = 2KE ÷ v²
  • v = √(2KE ÷ m)

💡 SI Unit: Joule (J)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Square the velocity before multiplying.

Potential Energy

PE = mgh

📖 Meaning: Potential energy is the energy stored due to height.

🎯 When to Use: Used for objects raised above the ground.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • PE = Mass × g × Height
  • m = PE ÷ gh
  • h = PE ÷ mg

💡 SI Unit: Joule (J)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Use g = 9.8 m/s² (or 10 m/s² if instructed).

Power

P = W / t

📖 Meaning: Power is the rate of doing work.

🎯 When to Use: Used to calculate power, work or time.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Power = Work ÷ Time
  • Work = Power × Time
  • Time = Work ÷ Power

💡 SI Unit: Watt (W)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Time must be in seconds.

Mechanical Efficiency

Efficiency = (Useful Output Energy / Input Energy) × 100%

📖 Meaning: Efficiency measures how effectively energy is converted.

🎯 When to Use: Used for machines and energy conversion problems.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Efficiency = (Output ÷ Input) × 100%
  • Output = (Efficiency × Input) ÷ 100
  • Input = (Output × 100) ÷ Efficiency

💡 SI Unit: Percentage (%)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Efficiency can never exceed 100%.

📂 Momentum and Collisions

Linear Momentum

p = mv

📖 Meaning: Momentum is the product of an object's mass and velocity.

🎯 When to Use: Used to calculate momentum, mass or velocity.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Momentum = Mass × Velocity
  • Mass = Momentum ÷ Velocity
  • Velocity = Momentum ÷ Mass

💡 SI Unit: kg·m/s

⚠️ Common Mistake: Velocity has direction, so momentum is a vector quantity.

Law of Conservation of Momentum

m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂

📖 Meaning: Total momentum before a collision equals the total momentum after the collision.

🎯 When to Use: Used in collision and explosion problems.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Total Initial Momentum = Total Final Momentum
  • Solve for any unknown velocity or mass.

💡 SI Unit: kg·m/s

⚠️ Common Mistake: Assign positive and negative signs correctly according to direction.

Impulse

J = Δp = Ft

📖 Meaning: Impulse equals the change in momentum produced by a force acting over time.

🎯 When to Use: Used in impact, collision and braking problems.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Impulse = Force × Time
  • Force = Impulse ÷ Time
  • Time = Impulse ÷ Force

💡 SI Unit: Newton-second (N·s) or kg·m/s

⚠️ Common Mistake: Use the change in momentum (final − initial), not just the final momentum.

Rate of Change of Momentum

F = Δp / Δt

📖 Meaning: Force is equal to the rate of change of momentum.

🎯 When to Use: Used when momentum changes over a known time interval.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Force = Change in Momentum ÷ Time
  • Δp = Force × Time
  • Δt = Change in Momentum ÷ Force

💡 SI Unit: Newton (N)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Use the time taken for the momentum to change, not the total time of motion.

Perfectly Inelastic Collision

v = (m₁u₁ + m₂u₂) / (m₁ + m₂)

📖 Meaning: Calculates the common velocity when two objects stick together after collision.

🎯 When to Use: Used in perfectly inelastic collision questions.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Common Velocity = Total Initial Momentum ÷ Total Mass

💡 SI Unit: metre per second (m/s)

⚠️ Common Mistake: This formula applies only when the objects move together after collision.

📂 Pressure

Pressure

P = F / A

📖 Meaning: Pressure is the force acting per unit area.

🎯 When to Use: Used to calculate pressure, force or area.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Pressure = Force ÷ Area
  • Force = Pressure × Area
  • Area = Force ÷ Pressure

💡 SI Unit: Pascal (Pa)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Area must always be in square metres (m²).

Liquid Pressure

P = ρgh

📖 Meaning: Pressure in a liquid depends on its density, depth and gravitational acceleration.

🎯 When to Use: Used for pressure at a given depth in liquids.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Pressure = Density × g × Height
  • Density = Pressure ÷ gh
  • Height = Pressure ÷ ρg

💡 SI Unit: Pascal (Pa)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Use the vertical depth, not the length of the container.

Hydraulic Press (Pascal's Principle)

F₁ / A₁ = F₂ / A₂

📖 Meaning: Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally in all directions.

🎯 When to Use: Used in hydraulic lifts, jacks and braking systems.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • F₁ = (A₁ × F₂) ÷ A₂
  • F₂ = (A₂ × F₁) ÷ A₁
  • A₁ / A₂ = F₁ / F₂

💡 SI Unit: Force (N), Area (m²)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Ensure both areas are in the same unit.

Upthrust (Buoyant Force)

U = ρgV

📖 Meaning: Upthrust equals the weight of the fluid displaced by an object.

🎯 When to Use: Used in floating and sinking problems.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Upthrust = Density × g × Volume
  • Volume = Upthrust ÷ ρg
  • Density = Upthrust ÷ gV

💡 SI Unit: Newton (N)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Use the volume of fluid displaced, not necessarily the object's total volume.

Relative Density

Relative Density = Density of Substance / Density of Water

📖 Meaning: Compares the density of a substance with the density of water.

🎯 When to Use: Used to determine whether an object will float or sink.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Relative Density = ρₛ ÷ ρw
  • Density of Substance = Relative Density × Density of Water

💡 SI Unit: No unit (dimensionless).

⚠️ Common Mistake: Relative density has no SI unit.

📂 Elasticity

Hooke's Law

F = kx

📖 Meaning: The extension of a spring is directly proportional to the applied force, provided the elastic limit is not exceeded.

🎯 When to Use: Used to calculate force, spring constant or extension.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Force = Spring Constant × Extension
  • Spring Constant = Force ÷ Extension
  • Extension = Force ÷ Spring Constant

💡 SI Unit: Spring Constant (N/m), Extension (m)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Extension must be in metres (m), not centimetres.

Stress

Stress = F / A

📖 Meaning: Stress is the force acting per unit cross-sectional area.

🎯 When to Use: Used in stretching and compression problems.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Stress = Force ÷ Area
  • Force = Stress × Area
  • Area = Force ÷ Stress

💡 SI Unit: Pascal (Pa) or N/m²

⚠️ Common Mistake: Use the cross-sectional area, not the surface area.

Strain

Strain = ΔL / L

📖 Meaning: Strain is the ratio of extension to the original length.

🎯 When to Use: Used to determine deformation in materials.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Strain = Extension ÷ Original Length
  • Extension = Strain × Original Length
  • Original Length = Extension ÷ Strain

💡 SI Unit: No unit (dimensionless)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Both lengths must be in the same unit.

Young's Modulus

E = Stress / Strain

📖 Meaning: Young's modulus measures the stiffness of a material.

🎯 When to Use: Used to compare the elasticity of different materials.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Young's Modulus = Stress ÷ Strain
  • Stress = E × Strain
  • Strain = Stress ÷ E

💡 SI Unit: Pascal (Pa)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Ensure strain has no unit before substitution.

Elastic Potential Energy

E = ½kx²

📖 Meaning: Energy stored in an elastic object due to stretching or compression.

🎯 When to Use: Used for springs and elastic materials.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Elastic Energy = ½ × Spring Constant × Extension²
  • k = 2E ÷ x²
  • x = √(2E ÷ k)

💡 SI Unit: Joule (J)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Remember to square the extension (x²).

📂 Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)

Time Period of a Pendulum

T = 2π √(L / g)

📖 Meaning: The time taken for one complete oscillation of a simple pendulum.

🎯 When to Use: Used to calculate the period, length or gravitational acceleration.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Time Period = 2π√(L/g)
  • L = g(T/2π)²
  • g = 4π²L/T²

💡 SI Unit: Time (s), Length (m)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Use the length of the pendulum, not the height of the bob.

Time Period of a Mass-Spring System

T = 2π √(m / k)

📖 Meaning: The time taken for one complete oscillation of a spring-mass system.

🎯 When to Use: Used for oscillating spring problems.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • T = 2π√(m/k)
  • m = k(T/2π)²
  • k = 4π²m/T²

💡 SI Unit: Time (s)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Mass must be in kilograms (kg).

Frequency

f = 1 / T

📖 Meaning: Frequency is the number of oscillations per second.

🎯 When to Use: Used when the time period is known.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Frequency = 1 ÷ Time Period
  • Time Period = 1 ÷ Frequency

💡 SI Unit: Hertz (Hz)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Frequency and time period are reciprocals.

Angular Frequency

ω = 2πf = 2π / T

📖 Meaning: Angular frequency is the rate of angular oscillation.

🎯 When to Use: Used in SHM and wave calculations.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • ω = 2πf
  • f = ω / 2π
  • T = 2π / ω

💡 SI Unit: rad/s

⚠️ Common Mistake: Do not confuse angular frequency with ordinary frequency.

Maximum Velocity in SHM

vₘₐₓ = ωA

📖 Meaning: The maximum speed of a particle performing SHM.

🎯 When to Use: Used when angular frequency and amplitude are known.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Maximum Velocity = Angular Frequency × Amplitude
  • ω = vₘₐₓ ÷ A
  • A = vₘₐₓ ÷ ω

💡 SI Unit: m/s

⚠️ Common Mistake: Amplitude must be in metres (m).

📂 Gravitation

Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation

F = Gm₁m₂ / r²

📖 Meaning: The gravitational force between two masses is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

🎯 When to Use: Used to calculate gravitational force between two objects.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • F = Gm₁m₂ ÷ r²
  • r = √(Gm₁m₂ ÷ F)
  • m₁ = Fr² ÷ Gm₂

💡 SI Unit: Force (N)

⚠️ Common Mistake: The distance r is measured between the centres of the two masses.

Gravitational Field Strength

g = F / m

📖 Meaning: Gravitational field strength is the force per unit mass.

🎯 When to Use: Used to calculate gravitational field strength, force or mass.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • g = F ÷ m
  • F = mg
  • m = F ÷ g

💡 SI Unit: N/kg or m/s²

⚠️ Common Mistake: Do not confuse gravitational field strength (g) with the gravitational constant (G).

Acceleration Due to Gravity

g = GM / r²

📖 Meaning: Calculates the acceleration due to gravity at a distance from the centre of a planet.

🎯 When to Use: Used in planetary motion and satellite problems.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • g = GM ÷ r²
  • M = gr² ÷ G
  • r = √(GM ÷ g)

💡 SI Unit: m/s²

⚠️ Common Mistake: Use the planet's radius from its centre, not its diameter.

Gravitational Potential Energy

PE = mgh

📖 Meaning: The energy possessed by an object due to its height above a reference level.

🎯 When to Use: Used near the Earth's surface.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • PE = mgh
  • m = PE ÷ gh
  • h = PE ÷ mg

💡 SI Unit: Joule (J)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Use g = 9.8 m/s² (or 10 m/s² if instructed).

Escape Velocity

v = √(2GM / r)

📖 Meaning: The minimum velocity required for an object to escape a planet's gravitational field.

🎯 When to Use: Used in space and satellite calculations.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • v = √(2GM ÷ r)
  • r = 2GM ÷ v²

💡 SI Unit: m/s

⚠️ Common Mistake: Use the radius from the planet's centre.

📂 Heat and Temperature

Heat Energy

Q = mcΔT

📖 Meaning: Heat energy required to change the temperature of a substance depends on its mass, specific heat capacity and temperature change.

🎯 When to Use: Used to calculate heat energy gained or lost.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Heat Energy = Mass × Specific Heat Capacity × Temperature Change
  • m = Q ÷ cΔT
  • c = Q ÷ mΔT
  • ΔT = Q ÷ mc

💡 SI Unit: Joule (J)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Temperature change must be in °C or K difference, not the final temperature.

Specific Heat Capacity

c = Q / mΔT

📖 Meaning: Specific heat capacity is the heat energy needed to raise 1 kg of a substance by 1°C.

🎯 When to Use: Used to compare how substances absorb heat.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Specific Heat Capacity = Heat Energy ÷ (Mass × Temperature Change)
  • Q = mcΔT
  • m = Q ÷ cΔT

💡 SI Unit: J/kg°C

⚠️ Common Mistake: Mass must be in kilograms.

Latent Heat

Q = mL

📖 Meaning: Heat energy required to change the state of a substance without changing its temperature.

🎯 When to Use: Used during melting, freezing, boiling or condensation.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Heat Energy = Mass × Specific Latent Heat
  • L = Q ÷ m
  • m = Q ÷ L

💡 SI Unit: Joule (J)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Latent heat involves a change of state, not temperature change.

Temperature Conversion

K = °C + 273

📖 Meaning: Converts temperature from Celsius scale to Kelvin scale.

🎯 When to Use: Used in gas laws and scientific calculations.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Kelvin = Celsius + 273
  • °C = K − 273

💡 SI Unit: Kelvin (K)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Kelvin does not use the degree symbol (write K, not °K).

Thermal Equilibrium

Heat Lost = Heat Gained

📖 Meaning: In an isolated system, heat lost by a hot object equals heat gained by a cold object.

🎯 When to Use: Used in mixing and calorimetry problems.

📝 Formula:

  • m₁c₁ΔT₁ = m₂c₂ΔT₂

💡 Memory Tip: Hot object loses what cold object gains.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Use temperature changes, not final temperatures.

📂 Thermal Expansion

Linear Expansion

ΔL = αL₀ΔT

📖 Meaning: The increase in length of a solid when its temperature changes.

🎯 When to Use: Used for expansion of rods, wires and other solid materials.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Change in Length = Original Length × Coefficient of Expansion × Temperature Change
  • α = ΔL ÷ (L₀ΔT)
  • L₀ = ΔL ÷ (αΔT)

💡 SI Unit: Coefficient of expansion (K⁻¹)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Use the original length, not the expanded length.

Final Length After Expansion

L = L₀(1 + αΔT)

📖 Meaning: Gives the new length of a material after heating.

🎯 When to Use: Used when the final size after expansion is required.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Final Length = Original Length × (1 + αΔT)
  • L₀ = L ÷ (1 + αΔT)

💡 Memory Tip: Expansion adds to the original length.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Do not forget the "1 +" in the formula.

Area Expansion

ΔA = βA₀ΔT

📖 Meaning: The increase in surface area of a material due to temperature change.

🎯 When to Use: Used for sheets and flat surfaces.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Change in Area = Area × Area Expansion Coefficient × Temperature Change

💡 SI Unit: m²

⚠️ Common Mistake: Area expansion coefficient is approximately twice the linear coefficient.

Volume Expansion

ΔV = γV₀ΔT

📖 Meaning: The increase in volume of a substance due to heating.

🎯 When to Use: Used for solids, liquids and gases expanding in volume.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Change in Volume = Original Volume × Volume Expansion Coefficient × Temperature Change

💡 Memory Tip: Volume expansion affects three dimensions.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Do not confuse volume expansion with linear expansion.

📂 Gas Laws

Boyle's Law

P₁V₁ = P₂V₂

📖 Meaning: For a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature, pressure is inversely proportional to volume.

🎯 When to Use: Used when pressure and volume change while temperature remains constant.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • P₁ = P₂V₂ ÷ V₁
  • V₁ = P₂V₂ ÷ P₁
  • P₂ = P₁V₁ ÷ V₂

💡 Memory Tip: Pressure increases when volume decreases.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Temperature must remain constant.

Charles' Law

V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂

📖 Meaning: The volume of a gas is directly proportional to its temperature at constant pressure.

🎯 When to Use: Used when volume changes with temperature.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • V₁T₂ = V₂T₁
  • V₂ = V₁T₂ ÷ T₁
  • T₂ = V₂T₁ ÷ V₁

💡 Memory Tip: Higher temperature gives higher volume.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Temperature must be in Kelvin.

Pressure Law

P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂

📖 Meaning: Pressure of a fixed mass of gas is directly proportional to temperature at constant volume.

🎯 When to Use: Used when pressure changes with temperature.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • P₁T₂ = P₂T₁
  • P₂ = P₁T₂ ÷ T₁
  • T₂ = P₂T₁ ÷ P₁

💡 Memory Tip: Pressure rises when temperature rises.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Always convert °C to Kelvin.

Combined Gas Law

P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂

📖 Meaning: Combines pressure, volume and temperature relationships for a fixed gas mass.

🎯 When to Use: Used when pressure, volume and temperature all change.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • P₁V₁T₂ = P₂V₂T₁

💡 Memory Tip: Put pressure and volume together, temperature below.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Use Kelvin, not Celsius.

Ideal Gas Equation

PV = nRT

📖 Meaning: Relates pressure, volume, amount of gas and temperature.

🎯 When to Use: Used for ideal gas calculations.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • P = nRT ÷ V
  • V = nRT ÷ P
  • T = PV ÷ nR

💡 SI Unit: Pressure (Pa), Volume (m³), Temperature (K)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Use the correct gas constant R.

v = fλ

Echo Distance

Distance = vt / 2

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Distance = (Speed × Time) ÷ 2
  • Speed = 2Distance ÷ Time

Sound Intensity Level

β = 10 log(I/I₀)

Frequency of a Vibrating String

f = 1/2L √(T/μ)

📖 Meaning: Beat frequency is the difference between two sound frequencies.

Beat frequency = 4 Hz

💡 Memory Tip: Beats = difference between frequencies.

📂 Light (Reflection & Refraction)

Law of Reflection

Angle of Incidence = Angle of Reflection i = r

📖 Meaning: The angle at which light hits a surface equals the angle at which it reflects.

🎯 When to Use: Used in reflection of light by mirrors.

📝 Example: If angle of incidence is 40°, angle of reflection is also 40°.

💡 Memory Tip: i = r (Incident equals Reflected).

⚠️ Common Mistake: Angles are measured from the normal, not the surface.

Refractive Index

n = c / v

📖 Meaning: Refractive index compares the speed of light in vacuum with its speed in a medium.

🎯 When to Use: Used to calculate how much light slows down in a material.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Refractive Index = Speed of Light in Vacuum ÷ Speed in Medium
  • v = c ÷ n
  • c = nv

💡 SI Unit: No unit (dimensionless).

⚠️ Common Mistake: Refractive index has no unit.

Snell's Law

n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r

📖 Meaning: Describes how light bends when it passes between different media.

🎯 When to Use: Used in refraction calculations.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • n₁ = n₂ sin r ÷ sin i
  • n₂ = n₁ sin i ÷ sin r

💡 Memory Tip: Light bends towards the normal when entering a denser medium.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Use angles measured from the normal.

Critical Angle

sin C = 1/n

📖 Meaning: The critical angle is the angle of incidence that produces an angle of refraction of 90°.

🎯 When to Use: Used in total internal reflection problems.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • n = 1/sin C

💡 Memory Tip: Critical angle is only for light moving from denser to less dense medium.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Do not use critical angle for any direction of light travel.

Lens Power

P = 1/f

📖 Meaning: Lens power measures how strongly a lens converges or diverges light.

🎯 When to Use: Used for lenses and optical instruments.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Power = 1 ÷ Focal Length

💡 SI Unit: Dioptre (D)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Focal length must be in metres.

📂 Lenses and Optical Instruments

Lens Formula

1/f = 1/v + 1/u

Magnification Using Heights

m = hᵢ / hₒ

Power of a Lens

P = 1/f

Compound Microscope Magnification

M = Mₒ × Mₑ

📖 Meaning: Total magnification is the product of objective lens magnification and eyepiece magnification.

📂 Electrostatics

Coulomb's Law

F = kq₁q₂ / r²

📖 Meaning: The electrostatic force between two charges depends on the size of the charges and the distance between them.

🎯 When to Use: Used to calculate force between two charged particles.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Force = k × Charge₁ × Charge₂ ÷ Distance²
  • q₁ = Fr² ÷ kq₂
  • r = √(kq₁q₂ ÷ F)

💡 SI Unit: Newton (N)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Distance must be in metres and charges in coulombs.

Electric Field Strength

E = F / Q

📖 Meaning: Electric field strength is the force experienced per unit charge.

🎯 When to Use: Used to calculate electric field, force or charge.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Electric Field = Force ÷ Charge
  • Force = Electric Field × Charge
  • Charge = Force ÷ Electric Field

💡 SI Unit: N/C or V/m

⚠️ Common Mistake: Do not confuse electric field strength with electric force.

Electric Potential Difference

V = W / Q

📖 Meaning: Potential difference is the work done per unit charge.

🎯 When to Use: Used to calculate voltage, work or charge.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Voltage = Work ÷ Charge
  • Work = Voltage × Charge
  • Charge = Work ÷ Voltage

💡 SI Unit: Volt (V)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Work must be measured in joules and charge in coulombs.

Electric Field Around a Point Charge

E = kQ / r²

📖 Meaning: Gives the electric field strength produced by a point charge.

🎯 When to Use: Used for isolated charges.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Q = Er² ÷ k
  • r = √(kQ ÷ E)

💡 SI Unit: N/C

⚠️ Common Mistake: Remember that field strength decreases with distance squared.

Capacitance

C = Q / V

📖 Meaning: Capacitance is the charge stored per unit potential difference.

🎯 When to Use: Used in capacitor calculations.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Charge = Capacitance × Voltage
  • Voltage = Charge ÷ Capacitance

💡 SI Unit: Farad (F)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Capacitance is not the same as charge.

📂 Magnetism

Force on a Current-Carrying Conductor

F = BIL sinθ

📖 Meaning: The force on a conductor carrying current in a magnetic field depends on magnetic field strength, current, length and angle.

🎯 When to Use: Used to calculate force on a wire in a magnetic field.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Force = Magnetic Field × Current × Length × sinθ
  • B = F ÷ ILsinθ
  • I = F ÷ BLsinθ

💡 SI Unit: Newton (N)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Use θ as the angle between the conductor and magnetic field.

Magnetic Flux

Φ = BA cosθ

📖 Meaning: Magnetic flux is the amount of magnetic field passing through an area.

🎯 When to Use: Used in electromagnetic induction calculations.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Flux = Magnetic Field × Area × cosθ
  • B = Φ ÷ A cosθ
  • A = Φ ÷ B cosθ

💡 SI Unit: Weber (Wb)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Maximum flux occurs when the field is perpendicular to the area.

Magnetic Field Strength Around a Straight Wire

B = μ₀I / 2πr

📖 Meaning: Calculates the magnetic field produced by current flowing through a straight conductor.

🎯 When to Use: Used for magnetic fields around wires.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • B = μ₀I ÷ 2πr
  • I = 2πrB ÷ μ₀

💡 Memory Tip: Magnetic field increases with current and decreases with distance.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Distance must be measured from the wire.

Magnetic Force Between Parallel Conductors

F/L = μ₀I₁I₂ / 2πr

📖 Meaning: Gives the force per unit length between two current-carrying parallel wires.

🎯 When to Use: Used for interaction between parallel conductors.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Force per length = μ₀ × Current₁ × Current₂ ÷ 2πr

💡 Memory Tip: Same current direction attracts, opposite direction repels.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Do not forget the length factor when finding total force.

Force on a Moving Charge in a Magnetic Field

F = Bqv sinθ

📖 Meaning: A moving charge experiences a force when it enters a magnetic field.

🎯 When to Use: Used for charged particles moving in magnetic fields.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Force = Magnetic Field × Charge × Velocity × sinθ
  • q = F ÷ Bv sinθ

💡 SI Unit: Newton (N)

⚠️ Common Mistake: No magnetic force acts when the charge moves parallel to the field.

📂 Electromagnetic Induction

Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction

E = -N ΔΦ / Δt

📖 Meaning: The induced electromotive force is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux linkage.

🎯 When to Use: Used to calculate induced voltage in coils and generators.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Induced EMF = Number of Turns × Change in Flux ÷ Time
  • N = EΔt ÷ ΔΦ
  • Δt = NΔΦ ÷ E

💡 Memory Tip: More turns and faster change produce more EMF.

⚠️ Common Mistake: The negative sign shows direction (Lenz's law), not negative voltage.

Magnetic Flux Linkage

NΦ

📖 Meaning: Total magnetic flux passing through all turns of a coil.

🎯 When to Use: Used when calculating induced EMF in coils.

📝 Formula:

  • Flux Linkage = Number of Turns × Magnetic Flux

💡 SI Unit: Weber-turn (Wb)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Remember to multiply by the number of turns.

Motional EMF

E = Blv

📖 Meaning: EMF produced when a conductor moves through a magnetic field.

🎯 When to Use: Used for moving wires in magnetic fields.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • EMF = Magnetic Field × Length × Velocity
  • B = E ÷ lv
  • v = E ÷ Bl

💡 SI Unit: Volt (V)

⚠️ Common Mistake: The conductor must cut magnetic field lines.

Transformer Equation

Vₚ/Vₛ = Nₚ/Nₛ

📖 Meaning: The voltage ratio of a transformer equals the ratio of the number of turns in the coils.

🎯 When to Use: Used for step-up and step-down transformers.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Vₛ = Vₚ × Nₛ ÷ Nₚ
  • Nₛ = VₛNₚ ÷ Vₚ

💡 Memory Tip: More turns give higher voltage.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Primary and secondary coils must not be mixed up.

Transformer Efficiency

Efficiency = (Pₒᵤₜ / Pᵢₙ) × 100%

📖 Meaning: Measures how much input power is converted into useful output power.

🎯 When to Use: Used to calculate transformer performance.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Efficiency = Output Power ÷ Input Power × 100
  • Output Power = Efficiency × Input Power

💡 Memory Tip: Efficiency can never exceed 100%.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Convert percentage correctly when calculating.

📂 Nuclear Physics

📖 Meaning: Mass can be converted into energy, and the energy produced is proportional to the mass lost.

Radioactive Decay Equation

N = N₀(1/2)ᵗ⁄ᵀ

Nuclear Activity

A = λN

📖 Meaning: Activity is the number of radioactive decays occurring per second.

🎯 When to Use: Used to calculate radioactive decay rate.

📝 Formula Rearrangement:

  • Activity = Decay Constant × Number of Nuclei
  • λ = A ÷ N
  • N = A ÷ λ

💡 SI Unit: Becquerel (Bq)

⚠️ Common Mistake: Activity measures decay rate, not amount of substance.
