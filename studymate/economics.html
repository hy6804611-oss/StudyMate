📈 ECONOMICS

Economics is the social science that studies how individuals, households, firms and governments make choices in allocating scarce resources to satisfy their unlimited wants. It explains how goods and services are produced, distributed and consumed, and how resources are managed to improve the standard of living.

📈 CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION TO ECONOMICS

Meaning of Economics

Economics is the study of how individuals and societies allocate scarce resources to satisfy unlimited human wants. It deals with the production, distribution, exchange and consumption of goods and services.

Definitions of Economics

Lionel Robbins' Definition – Economics is the science which studies human behaviour as a relationship between unlimited wants and scarce resources which have alternative uses.

Alfred Marshall's Definition – Economics is the study of mankind in the ordinary business of life.

Paul Samuelson's Definition – Economics is the study of how people and society choose to use scarce productive resources to produce goods and services and distribute them among members of society.

Nature of Economics

Economics is a Social Science – It studies human behaviour in relation to the use of scarce resources.

Economics is Dynamic – Economic principles change as society, technology and the economy develop.

Economics is Both a Science and an Art – It applies scientific methods to solve practical economic problems.

Economics is Concerned with Choice – It explains how people make decisions because resources are limited.

Basic Concepts in Economics

Human Wants – Human wants are the desires for goods and services that give satisfaction.

Resources – Resources are the materials and services used to produce goods and services.

Scarcity – Scarcity is the condition where available resources are limited but human wants are unlimited.

Choice – Choice is the selection of one want instead of another because resources are scarce.

Opportunity Cost – Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative forgone when a choice is made.

Importance of Economics

• Helps individuals make wise economic decisions.

• Assists governments in making sound economic policies.

• Promotes efficient allocation of scarce resources.

• Improves business decision-making.

• Helps solve economic problems such as unemployment and inflation.

• Encourages economic growth and development.

• Improves the standard of living.

• Promotes proper management of income and expenditure.

Branches of Economics

Microeconomics – Studies the behaviour of individual consumers, households and firms.

Macroeconomics – Studies the economy as a whole, including national income, inflation, unemployment and economic growth.

⭐ Understand scarcity, choice and opportunity cost.

⭐ Differentiate between Microeconomics and Macroeconomics.

⭐ Learn the importance of Economics.

⭐ Be able to explain why Economics is regarded as a social science.

📈 CHAPTER 2: BASIC ECONOMIC PROBLEMS (SCARCITY, CHOICE, SCALE OF PREFERENCE & OPPORTUNITY COST)

The basic economic problems arise because human wants are unlimited while the resources available to satisfy those wants are limited. As a result, individuals, firms and governments must make choices on how best to use available resources.

Scarcity

Scarcity is the condition where the available resources are insufficient to satisfy all human wants. Since resources are limited, people cannot have everything they desire.

Causes of Scarcity

Limited Resources – Resources such as land, labour and capital are limited in supply.

Unlimited Human Wants – Human wants continue to increase and can never be completely satisfied.

Population Growth – An increase in population leads to higher demand for limited resources.

Poor Resource Management – Wasteful use of resources increases scarcity.

Choice

Choice is the act of selecting one want instead of another because available resources cannot satisfy all wants at the same time.

Scale of Preference

A scale of preference is a list of wants arranged in order of their importance. It helps individuals decide which wants should be satisfied first.

Opportunity Cost

Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative forgone when a choice is made. Whenever a person chooses one option, another valuable option is sacrificed.

Relationship Between Scarcity, Choice and Opportunity Cost

Scarcity forces people to make choices, and every choice involves an opportunity cost.

Basic Economic Problems

What to Produce – Society must decide which goods and services should be produced.

How to Produce – Society must choose the most efficient method of production.

For Whom to Produce – Society must determine who should receive the goods and services produced.

Importance of Making Economic Choices

• Ensures efficient use of scarce resources.

• Helps individuals satisfy their most important wants first.

• Reduces waste of resources.

• Promotes better planning and budgeting.

• Encourages rational decision-making.

Exam Tips

⭐ Understand the relationship between scarcity, choice and opportunity cost.

⭐ Memorize the three basic economic problems.

⭐ Be able to arrange wants using a scale of preference.

⭐ Expect objective questions on opportunity cost and scarcity.

📈 CHAPTER 3: FACTORS OF PRODUCTION

Factors of production are the resources used in the production of goods and services. They are the basic inputs required to produce goods and services that satisfy human wants.

Meaning of Factors of Production

Factors of production are the natural, human and man-made resources combined to produce goods and services. The four major factors of production are Land, Labour, Capital and Entrepreneur.

Land

Land refers to all the natural resources provided by nature that are used in the production process.

Examples of Land Resources – Forests, rivers, minerals, crude oil, fertile soil and oceans.

Reward for Land – Rent.

Characteristics of Land

It is a Free Gift of Nature – It is not created by man.

It is Fixed in Supply – The total quantity of land cannot be increased.

It is Immobile – Land cannot be moved from one place to another.

Its Quality Varies – Some land is more fertile or valuable than others.

Labour

Labour refers to all human physical and mental efforts used in producing goods and services.

Reward for Labour – Wages or Salaries.

Characteristics of Labour

Labour is Human Effort – It involves both physical and intellectual work.

Labour is Perishable – Unused labour cannot be stored for future use.

Efficiency Varies – Workers differ in skill, education and experience.

Labour is Mobile – Workers can move from one occupation or place to another.

Capital

Capital refers to all man-made assets used in producing other goods and services.

Examples of Capital – Machines, tools, buildings, vehicles and equipment.

Reward for Capital – Interest.

Characteristics of Capital

It is Man-made – Capital is created by human effort.

It Increases Productivity – It helps produce goods faster and more efficiently.

It Depreciates – Capital wears out with use over time.

Entrepreneur

An entrepreneur is the person who organizes and combines the other factors of production, takes business risks and makes important business decisions.

Reward for Entrepreneur – Profit.

Functions of an Entrepreneur

• Organizes the factors of production.

• Makes business decisions.

• Bears business risks.

• Provides capital.

• Introduces innovation.

• Coordinates production activities.

Importance of Factors of Production

• They make production possible.

• They create employment opportunities.

• They promote economic growth.

• They improve the standard of living.

• They increase national income.

Exam Tips

⭐ Know the four factors of production.

⭐ Memorize the reward for each factor of production.

⭐ Understand the characteristics of Land, Labour, Capital and Entrepreneur.

⭐ Learn the functions of an entrepreneur.

⭐ Be able to identify examples of each factor of production.

📈 CHAPTER 4: PRODUCTION

Production is the process of creating or increasing the usefulness of goods and services to satisfy human wants. It involves combining the factors of production to produce goods and services.

Meaning of Production

Production is the transformation of raw materials into finished goods and the provision of services that satisfy human wants.

Types of Production

Primary Production – This involves the extraction of natural resources from the earth.

Examples – Farming, fishing, mining, forestry and hunting.

Secondary Production – This involves converting raw materials into finished or semi-finished goods.

Examples – Manufacturing, construction and food processing.

Tertiary Production – This involves the provision of services to individuals and businesses.

Examples – Banking, transportation, education, healthcare and communication.

Levels of Production

Subsistence Production – Production mainly for personal or family consumption.

Commercial Production – Production mainly for sale and profit.

Factors Affecting Production

Availability of Capital – Adequate capital increases production.

Labour – Skilled and efficient workers improve production.

Technology – Modern technology increases productivity.

Government Policies – Taxation, subsidies and regulations influence production.

Availability of Raw Materials – Sufficient raw materials encourage production.

Entrepreneurial Ability – Good management leads to efficient production.

Importance of Production

• Satisfies human wants.

• Creates employment opportunities.

• Generates income for producers.

• Increases the supply of goods and services.

• Promotes economic growth and development.

• Improves the standard of living.

• Increases national income.

Production and Productivity

Production refers to the total output of goods and services.

Productivity refers to the efficiency with which resources are used to produce goods and services.

Higher productivity means producing more output with the same amount of resources.

📈 CHAPTER 5: DIVISION OF LABOUR AND SPECIALIZATION

Division of labour and specialization are important concepts in Economics that increase efficiency and productivity. They involve breaking down production into different tasks and allowing individuals or firms to concentrate on specific activities.

Meaning of Division of Labour

Division of labour is the process of breaking a production activity into smaller tasks, with each worker performing a specific task according to his or her skill or ability.

Meaning of Specialization

Specialization is the concentration of an individual, firm, region or country on the production of a particular good or service in which it has the greatest advantage.

Types of Specialization

Occupational Specialization – Individuals concentrate on specific professions such as teaching, medicine or engineering.

Industrial Specialization – Firms specialize in producing particular goods or services.

Regional Specialization – Different regions concentrate on producing goods they have comparative advantages in.

International Specialization – Countries specialize in producing goods they can produce more efficiently than others.

Advantages of Division of Labour

• Increases productivity.

• Saves production time.

• Improves workers' skills through repetition.

• Encourages the use of machines.

• Reduces production costs.

• Promotes mass production.

• Improves the quality of goods.

• Increases profits.

Disadvantages of Division of Labour

• Work becomes monotonous and boring.

• Workers become over-specialized.

• High unemployment may occur if one stage stops.

• Heavy dependence on machines.

• It may reduce workers' creativity.

• Production may stop if one worker fails to perform a task.

Relationship Between Division of Labour and Specialization

Division of labour makes specialization possible by assigning different tasks to different workers, while specialization improves efficiency by allowing each worker to focus on one task.

Importance of Specialization

• Encourages efficiency.

• Promotes economic growth.

• Increases output.

• Improves the standard of living.

• Encourages international trade.

Exam Tips

⭐ Know the meanings of division of labour and specialization.

⭐ Differentiate between division of labour and specialization.

⭐ Memorize the advantages and disadvantages of division of labour.

⭐ Learn the different types of specialization.

⭐ Be able to explain how specialization increases productivity.

📈 CHAPTER 6: EXCHANGE

Exchange is the process of giving one good, service or money in return for another. It enables individuals, businesses and nations to obtain the goods and services they need. Exchange is an essential part of economic activities because production is meaningful only when goods and services are exchanged for consumption.

Meaning of Exchange

Exchange is the transfer of goods and services between buyers and sellers for money or for other goods and services.

Forms of Exchange

Barter System – The direct exchange of one good or service for another without the use of money.

Money Exchange – The exchange of goods and services using money as a medium of exchange.

Barter System

The barter system was the earliest form of exchange where goods and services were exchanged directly without using money.

Advantages of Barter System

• It does not require money.

• It is useful where money is unavailable.

• It encourages direct exchange of goods.

Disadvantages of Barter System

Double Coincidence of Wants – Both parties must want what the other has before exchange can take place.

No Common Measure of Value – It is difficult to determine the value of different goods.

Difficulty in Storing Wealth – Some goods are perishable and cannot be stored for long.

Difficulty in Deferred Payments – Future payments cannot easily be agreed upon.

Lack of Divisibility – Some goods cannot be divided without losing value.

Money Exchange

Money exchange is the modern system where money is used as a medium of exchange to buy and sell goods and services.

Advantages of Money Exchange

• It eliminates the problem of double coincidence of wants.

• It provides a common measure of value.

• It makes buying and selling easier.

• It serves as a store of value.

• It allows deferred payments.

• It promotes local and international trade.

Importance of Exchange

• Facilitates trade and commerce.

• Encourages specialization.

• Increases production.

• Improves the standard of living.

• Promotes economic growth and development.

• Creates employment opportunities.

Exam Tips

⭐ Know the meaning of exchange.

⭐ Differentiate between barter system and money exchange.

⭐ Memorize the advantages and disadvantages of the barter system.

⭐ Understand why money replaced the barter system.

⭐ Be able to explain the importance of exchange in an economy.

📈 CHAPTER 7: DEMAND

Demand is one of the fundamental concepts in Economics. It refers to the quantity of a good or service that consumers are willing and able to buy at different prices during a given period of time. Demand is not just the desire for a commodity but also the ability and willingness to pay for it.

Meaning of Demand

Demand is the quantity of a commodity that consumers are willing and able to purchase at various prices within a given period of time.

Effective Demand

Effective demand is the willingness and ability of consumers to buy a good or service at a particular price.

Law of Demand

The Law of Demand states that, all other things being equal, the higher the price of a commodity, the lower the quantity demanded, and the lower the price, the higher the quantity demanded.

Demand Schedule

A demand schedule is a table showing the different quantities of a commodity that consumers are willing and able to buy at different prices.

Demand Curve

A demand curve is a graphical representation of the relationship between the price of a commodity and the quantity demanded. It slopes downward from left to right because of the inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded.

Factors Affecting Demand

Price of the Commodity – As price increases, demand decreases, and vice versa.

Income of Consumers – Higher income usually increases demand for normal goods.

Price of Related Goods – The prices of substitute and complementary goods affect demand.

Taste and Fashion – Changes in consumers' preferences influence demand.

Population – An increase in population generally increases demand.

Season and Weather – Certain goods are demanded more during particular seasons.

Advertisement – Effective advertising can increase demand.

Expectation of Future Prices – Consumers may buy more if they expect prices to rise in the future.

Movement Along the Demand Curve

Extension of Demand – An increase in quantity demanded due to a fall in price.

Contraction of Demand – A decrease in quantity demanded due to a rise in price.

Shift in Demand Curve

Increase in Demand – Caused by factors other than price, such as higher income or population growth.

Decrease in Demand – Caused by factors such as lower income, reduced population or unfavourable changes in taste.

Importance of Demand

• Helps producers determine what to produce.

• Assists businesses in fixing prices.

• Guides government in economic planning.

• Helps predict consumer behaviour.

• Assists in resource allocation.

Exam Tips

⭐ Know the meaning of demand and effective demand.

⭐ Understand the Law of Demand.

⭐ Differentiate between movement along the demand curve and shift in the demand curve.

⭐ Memorize the factors affecting demand.

⭐ Be able to interpret a demand schedule and demand curve.

📈 CHAPTER 8: SUPPLY

Supply is one of the basic concepts in Economics. It refers to the quantity of a good or service that producers are willing and able to offer for sale at different prices during a given period of time. Generally, producers supply more goods when prices are high and less when prices are low.

Meaning of Supply

Supply is the quantity of a commodity that producers are willing and able to offer for sale at various prices within a given period of time.

Law of Supply

The Law of Supply states that, all other things being equal, the higher the price of a commodity, the greater the quantity supplied, and the lower the price, the smaller the quantity supplied.

Supply Schedule

A supply schedule is a table showing the different quantities of a commodity that producers are willing and able to supply at different prices.

Supply Curve

A supply curve is a graphical representation of the relationship between the price of a commodity and the quantity supplied. It slopes upward from left to right because of the direct relationship between price and quantity supplied.

Factors Affecting Supply

Price of the Commodity – Higher prices encourage producers to supply more goods.

Cost of Production – An increase in production costs reduces supply.

Technology – Improved technology increases supply by making production more efficient.

Government Policies – Taxes reduce supply while subsidies increase supply.

Price of Related Goods – Producers may switch to producing more profitable goods.

Number of Producers – More producers in the market increase supply.

Natural Factors – Weather, climate and natural disasters affect the supply of agricultural products.

Future Price Expectations – Producers may reduce current supply if they expect higher prices in the future.

Movement Along the Supply Curve

Extension of Supply – An increase in quantity supplied due to an increase in price.

Contraction of Supply – A decrease in quantity supplied due to a fall in price.

Shift in the Supply Curve

Increase in Supply – Caused by factors other than price, such as improved technology or lower production costs.

Decrease in Supply – Caused by factors such as higher production costs, heavy taxation or natural disasters.

Importance of Supply

• Helps determine market prices.

• Guides producers in production planning.

• Assists governments in economic planning.

• Promotes efficient allocation of resources.

• Helps satisfy consumers' wants.

Exam Tips

⭐ Know the meaning of supply.

⭐ Understand the Law of Supply.

⭐ Differentiate between extension and contraction of supply.

⭐ Distinguish between movement along the supply curve and shift in the supply curve.

⭐ Memorize the factors affecting supply and be able to explain them.

📈 CHAPTER 9: PRICE DETERMINATION (EQUILIBRIUM PRICE)

Price determination is the process through which the price of a commodity is established by the interaction of demand and supply in the market. The price at which the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied is known as the equilibrium price.

Meaning of Price Determination

Price determination refers to the process by which the forces of demand and supply interact to fix the market price of goods and services.

Equilibrium Price

Equilibrium price is the price at which the quantity demanded by consumers is exactly equal to the quantity supplied by producers. At this point, there is neither excess demand nor excess supply.

Equilibrium Quantity

Equilibrium quantity is the quantity of a commodity bought and sold at the equilibrium price.

Conditions for Equilibrium

Quantity Demanded Equals Quantity Supplied – Buyers are willing to buy exactly what sellers are willing to sell.

No Pressure on Price – The market price remains stable because there is no shortage or surplus.

Excess Demand (Shortage)

Excess demand occurs when the quantity demanded is greater than the quantity supplied at a particular price. This usually leads to an increase in price.

Excess Supply (Surplus)

Excess supply occurs when the quantity supplied is greater than the quantity demanded at a particular price. This usually leads to a fall in price.

Factors Affecting Price Determination

Demand – An increase in demand tends to increase price.

Supply – An increase in supply tends to reduce price.

Government Policies – Price control, taxes and subsidies affect market prices.

Cost of Production – Higher production costs often lead to higher prices.

Competition – Increased competition may reduce prices.

Consumer Income – Changes in income can influence demand and market prices.

Importance of Price Determination

• Helps allocate scarce resources efficiently.

• Guides producers on what and how much to produce.

• Helps consumers make purchasing decisions.

• Promotes market stability.

• Assists governments in economic planning.

Price Control

Price control is the direct regulation of the prices of goods and services by the government in order to protect consumers or producers.

Maximum Price (Price Ceiling) – The highest price that can legally be charged for a commodity.

Minimum Price (Price Floor) – The lowest price that can legally be charged for a commodity.

Exam Tips

⭐ Know the meaning of price determination.

⭐ Understand equilibrium price and equilibrium quantity.

⭐ Differentiate between excess demand and excess supply.

⭐ Learn the factors affecting price determination.

⭐ Be able to explain maximum price and minimum price.

📈 CHAPTER 10: ELASTICITY OF DEMAND AND SUPPLY

Elasticity of demand and supply measures the degree of responsiveness of quantity demanded or quantity supplied to changes in factors such as price, income and the prices of related goods. It helps producers, consumers and governments understand how markets react to economic changes.

Meaning of Elasticity of Demand

Elasticity of demand is the degree of responsiveness of quantity demanded to changes in price, income or the prices of related goods.

Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)

Price Elasticity of Demand measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in the price of a commodity.

Types of Price Elasticity of Demand

Perfectly Elastic Demand – A very small change in price causes an infinite change in quantity demanded.

Perfectly Inelastic Demand – Quantity demanded remains unchanged regardless of changes in price.

Relatively Elastic Demand – Quantity demanded changes by a greater percentage than the change in price.

Relatively Inelastic Demand – Quantity demanded changes by a smaller percentage than the change in price.

Unitary Elastic Demand – The percentage change in quantity demanded is equal to the percentage change in price.

Factors Affecting Elasticity of Demand

Availability of Substitutes – Goods with close substitutes have more elastic demand.

Nature of the Commodity – Necessities usually have inelastic demand, while luxuries have elastic demand.

Proportion of Income Spent – Expensive goods tend to have more elastic demand.

Time – Demand becomes more elastic over a longer period.

Number of Uses – Goods with many uses usually have elastic demand.

Meaning of Elasticity of Supply

Elasticity of supply is the degree of responsiveness of quantity supplied to changes in the price of a commodity.

Types of Elasticity of Supply

Perfectly Elastic Supply – Producers are willing to supply any quantity at a particular price.

Perfectly Inelastic Supply – Quantity supplied remains constant regardless of price changes.

Relatively Elastic Supply – Quantity supplied changes by a greater percentage than the change in price.

Relatively Inelastic Supply – Quantity supplied changes by a smaller percentage than the change in price.

Unitary Elastic Supply – The percentage change in quantity supplied equals the percentage change in price.

Factors Affecting Elasticity of Supply

Availability of Raw Materials – Adequate raw materials increase elasticity of supply.

Length of Production Period – Goods that take longer to produce usually have inelastic supply.

Level of Technology – Better technology increases elasticity of supply.

Availability of Storage Facilities – Goods that can be stored easily usually have more elastic supply.

Number of Producers – More producers generally increase elasticity of supply.

Importance of Elasticity

• Helps producers determine pricing policies.

• Assists governments in taxation decisions.

• Helps businesses forecast sales.

• Guides firms in production planning.

• Assists consumers in making purchasing decisions.

Exam Tips

⭐ Know the meaning of elasticity of demand and elasticity of supply.

⭐ Memorize the five types of price elasticity of demand.

⭐ Learn the factors affecting elasticity of demand and supply.

⭐ Be able to distinguish between elastic and inelastic demand.

⭐ Expect objective questions on the types and importance of elasticity.

📈 CHAPTER 11: THEORY OF CONSUMER BEHAVIOUR

The Theory of Consumer Behaviour explains how consumers make decisions on the purchase of goods and services in order to obtain the greatest possible satisfaction from their limited income. Since human wants are unlimited and income is limited, consumers must make rational choices.

Meaning of Consumer Behaviour

Consumer behaviour refers to the study of how individuals or households decide what goods and services to buy, how much to buy and when to buy them in order to maximize satisfaction.

Meaning of Utility

Utility is the satisfaction or benefit a consumer derives from consuming a good or service.

Types of Utility

Form Utility – Utility created by changing the form of raw materials into finished goods.

Place Utility – Utility created by transporting goods from one place to another.

Time Utility – Utility created by storing goods until they are needed.

Possession Utility – Utility created by transferring ownership of goods from the seller to the buyer.

Total Utility (TU)

Total Utility is the total satisfaction a consumer obtains from consuming a given quantity of a commodity.

Marginal Utility (MU)

Marginal Utility is the additional satisfaction obtained from consuming one more unit of a commodity.

Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility

The Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility states that as a consumer continues to consume additional units of the same commodity, the extra satisfaction derived from each additional unit gradually decreases, other things being equal.

Consumer Equilibrium

Consumer equilibrium is the point at which a consumer obtains maximum satisfaction from available income and has no desire to change the pattern of consumption.

Assumptions of Consumer Behaviour Theory

Consumers are Rational – Consumers aim to maximize satisfaction.

Income is Limited – Consumers have limited resources.

Wants are Unlimited – Human wants can never be completely satisfied.

Consumers have Perfect Information – Consumers know the prices and quality of goods.

Factors Affecting Consumer Behaviour

Income – Higher income increases purchasing power.

Price of Goods – Consumers usually buy more when prices fall.

Taste and Preference – Personal likes and dislikes influence buying decisions.

Fashion – Changes in fashion affect demand for certain goods.

Advertisement – Effective advertising influences consumers' choices.

Availability of Substitute Goods – Consumers may switch to alternatives when prices rise.

Importance of Consumer Behaviour

• Helps consumers make rational decisions.

• Assists producers in understanding consumers' needs.

• Guides firms in pricing and marketing decisions.

• Promotes efficient allocation of limited income.

• Improves consumer satisfaction.

Exam Tips

⭐ Know the meanings of utility, total utility and marginal utility.

⭐ Understand the Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility.

⭐ Differentiate between Total Utility and Marginal Utility.

⭐ Learn the factors affecting consumer behaviour.

⭐ Be able to explain consumer equilibrium.

📈 CHAPTER 12: THEORY OF COST AND REVENUE

The Theory of Cost and Revenue explains the expenses incurred by firms during production and the income earned from the sale of goods and services. Every business aims to minimize costs and maximize revenue in order to earn profit.

Meaning of Cost

Cost is the total amount of money spent by a producer on the factors of production in the process of producing goods and services.

Types of Cost

Fixed Cost (FC) – Costs that do not change with the level of output. They remain constant whether production increases or decreases.

Examples of Fixed Costs – Rent, insurance, salaries of permanent staff and property taxes.

Variable Cost (VC) – Costs that change directly with the level of production.

Examples of Variable Costs – Raw materials, fuel, electricity for production and wages of casual workers.

Total Cost (TC) – The sum of Fixed Cost and Variable Cost.

Formula: Total Cost = Fixed Cost + Variable Cost (TC = FC + VC)

Average Cost (AC)

Average Cost is the cost of producing one unit of output.

Formula: Average Cost = Total Cost ÷ Total Output (AC = TC ÷ Q)

Marginal Cost (MC)

Marginal Cost is the additional cost incurred in producing one extra unit of output.

Meaning of Revenue

Revenue is the income a firm receives from the sale of goods and services.

Types of Revenue

Total Revenue (TR) – The total income received from the sale of all units of a commodity.

Formula: Total Revenue = Price × Quantity Sold (TR = P × Q)

Average Revenue (AR) – Revenue earned per unit of output sold.

Formula: Average Revenue = Total Revenue ÷ Quantity Sold (AR = TR ÷ Q)

Marginal Revenue (MR) – The additional revenue obtained from selling one extra unit of a commodity.

Meaning of Profit

Profit is the excess of total revenue over total cost.

Formula: Profit = Total Revenue − Total Cost (Profit = TR − TC)

Importance of Cost and Revenue Analysis

• Helps firms determine profit.

• Assists producers in fixing prices.

• Guides business planning and budgeting.

• Helps firms minimize production costs.

• Assists in decision-making.

• Promotes efficient management of business resources.

Exam Tips

⭐ Know the meanings of cost, revenue and profit.

⭐ Memorize the types of cost and revenue.

⭐ Understand the formulas for TC, AC, TR, AR and Profit.

⭐ Differentiate between Fixed Cost and Variable Cost.

⭐ Be able to solve simple objective questions involving cost and revenue calculations.

📈 CHAPTER 13: MARKET STRUCTURES

A market structure refers to the characteristics and organization of a market that determine the level of competition among firms. It explains how buyers and sellers interact in the production and sale of goods and services.

Meaning of Market

A market is any place or arrangement where buyers and sellers meet to exchange goods and services at an agreed price.

Meaning of Market Structure

Market structure refers to the nature and degree of competition in a market. It depends on the number of buyers and sellers, the type of products sold, ease of entry and exit, and the availability of information.

Types of Market Structure

Perfect Competition – A market with many buyers and sellers where no individual can influence the market price.

Monopoly – A market where there is only one producer or seller of a product with no close substitute.

Monopolistic Competition – A market with many firms selling similar but slightly differentiated products.

Oligopoly – A market dominated by a few large firms producing similar or differentiated products.

Characteristics of Perfect Competition

• Large number of buyers and sellers.

• Homogeneous (identical) products.

• Free entry and exit of firms.

• Perfect knowledge of market conditions.

• Firms are price takers.

Characteristics of Monopoly

• Only one producer or seller.

• No close substitutes.

• High barriers to entry.

• The monopolist is a price maker.

• Large control over market supply.

Characteristics of Monopolistic Competition

• Many buyers and sellers.

• Product differentiation.

• Free entry and exit.

• Heavy advertising and branding.

Characteristics of Oligopoly

• Few large firms dominate the market.

• High barriers to entry.

• Firms are interdependent.

• Products may be similar or differentiated.

• Intense competition through advertising.

Advantages of Perfect Competition

• Fair prices for consumers.

• Efficient allocation of resources.

• Encourages healthy competition.

• High-quality products.

Disadvantages of Monopoly

• High prices.

• Limited consumer choice.

• Exploitation of consumers.

• Reduced competition.

Importance of Market Structures

• Determines market prices.

• Influences production decisions.

• Promotes efficient resource allocation.

• Helps governments formulate competition policies.

• Influences consumer welfare.

Exam Tips

⭐ Know the meaning of market and market structure.

⭐ Differentiate between perfect competition, monopoly, monopolistic competition and oligopoly.

⭐ Memorize the characteristics of each market structure.

⭐ Know the advantages of perfect competition and the disadvantages of monopoly.

⭐ Be able to identify examples of different market structures.

📈 CHAPTER 14: BUSINESS ORGANIZATIONS

Business organizations are establishments created to produce goods and services for the satisfaction of human wants and to earn profits. They differ in terms of ownership, size, capital, management and legal status.

Meaning of Business Organization

A business organization is an enterprise established by individuals or groups to carry out commercial, industrial or professional activities for the purpose of making profit.

Types of Business Organizations

Sole Proprietorship – A business owned, financed and managed by one person.

Partnership – A business owned and managed by two or more persons who share profits and losses according to an agreed ratio.

Private Limited Liability Company – A company owned by private individuals with limited liability and whose shares are not offered to the general public.

📈 CHAPTER 15: MONEY

Money is anything that is generally accepted as a medium of exchange for goods and services and for the settlement of debts. It has replaced the barter system and has made trade easier and more efficient.

Meaning of Money

Money is anything that is generally accepted by people in exchange for goods and services and in the payment of debts.

Evolution of Money

Barter System – The direct exchange of goods and services without the use of money.

Commodity Money – Goods such as salt, cattle, cowries and tobacco used as money.

Metal Money – Coins made from metals such as gold, silver and copper.

Paper Money – Banknotes issued by the Central Bank.

Electronic Money – Money transferred through electronic means such as ATM cards, mobile banking and online banking.

Characteristics of Good Money

General Acceptability – It must be accepted by everyone.

Durability – It should last for a long time without damage.

Portability – It should be easy to carry from one place to another.

Divisibility – It should be capable of being divided into smaller units.

Uniformity – Units of money should be identical in quality and value.

Recognizability – It should be easy to identify and difficult to counterfeit.

Stability of Value – It should maintain its purchasing power over time.

Functions of Money

Medium of Exchange – It is used to buy and sell goods and services.

Measure of Value – It provides a common standard for determining prices.

Store of Value – It allows people to save wealth for future use.

Standard of Deferred Payment – It is used to settle debts in the future.

Transfer of Value – It makes it easy to transfer purchasing power from one person or place to another.

Importance of Money

• Facilitates trade and commerce.

• Eliminates the problems of the barter system.

• Encourages savings and investment.

• Promotes specialization.

• Makes accounting and record keeping easier.

• Supports economic growth and development.

Problems of Using Money

• Inflation reduces the value of money.

• Counterfeiting causes financial losses.

• Theft and robbery may occur.

• Poor management can lead to financial crises.

📈 CHAPTER 17: INFLATION

Inflation is the persistent and general increase in the prices of goods and services over a period of time. As prices rise, the purchasing power of money falls, meaning that the same amount of money can buy fewer goods and services.

Meaning of Inflation

Inflation is a continuous rise in the general price level of goods and services, leading to a decline in the value or purchasing power of money.

Types of Inflation

Creeping Inflation – A slow and gradual rise in the general price level.

Walking Inflation – A moderate increase in prices at a faster rate than creeping inflation.

Running Inflation – A rapid increase in prices that significantly affects the economy.

Hyperinflation – An extremely rapid and uncontrollable rise in prices over a short period.

Causes of Inflation

Excess Money Supply – Too much money chasing too few goods.

Increase in Demand – When demand exceeds supply, prices rise.

High Cost of Production – Rising costs of labour, raw materials and energy increase prices.

Government Deficit Financing – Excessive government borrowing and printing of money can cause inflation.

Shortage of Goods and Services – Limited supply leads to higher prices.

Natural Disasters – Floods, droughts and other disasters reduce production and increase prices.

Effects of Inflation

• Reduces the purchasing power of money.

• Increases the cost of living.

• Discourages savings.

• Reduces the value of fixed incomes.

• Creates uncertainty in business planning.

• May increase wages and production costs.

• Can lead to unemployment if businesses reduce production.

Measures to Control Inflation

Monetary Policy – The Central Bank reduces the money supply by increasing interest rates or selling government securities.

Fiscal Policy – The government reduces public spending and increases taxes.

Increase in Production – Producing more goods and services helps reduce shortages.

Importation of Goods – Importing essential goods increases supply and stabilizes prices.

Price Control – The government may regulate the prices of essential goods.

Importance of Price Stability

• Encourages investment.

• Promotes economic growth.

• Protects consumers' purchasing power.

• Encourages savings.

• Improves business confidence.

Exam Tips

⭐ Know the meaning of inflation.

⭐ Memorize the types of inflation.

⭐ Learn the causes and effects of inflation.

⭐ Understand the measures used to control inflation.

⭐ Be able to explain why inflation reduces the purchasing power of money.

📈 CHAPTER 20: UNEMPLOYMENT

Unemployment is a situation in which people who are willing and able to work at the prevailing wage rate cannot find suitable jobs. It is one of the major economic problems faced by many developing countries, including Nigeria.

Meaning of Unemployment

Unemployment refers to the condition where individuals who are qualified, willing and able to work are unable to secure paid employment.

Meaning of Labour Force

The labour force consists of all persons of working age who are employed or actively seeking employment.

Types of Unemployment

Frictional Unemployment – Temporary unemployment experienced by people changing jobs or entering the labour market.

Structural Unemployment – Unemployment caused by changes in technology or the structure of the economy.

Seasonal Unemployment – Unemployment resulting from seasonal changes in economic activities such as farming and tourism.

Cyclical Unemployment – Unemployment caused by economic recession or a decline in business activities.

Disguised Unemployment – A situation where more workers are employed than are actually needed, making some workers unproductive.

Voluntary Unemployment – When people choose not to work despite the availability of jobs.

Causes of Unemployment

Rapid Population Growth – The labour force grows faster than available jobs.

Poor Economic Growth – Slow economic development reduces job opportunities.

Lack of Skills – Many job seekers lack the qualifications required by employers.

Technological Advancement – Machines replace human labour in some industries.

Closure of Industries – Businesses shutting down lead to job losses.

Political Instability – Insecurity discourages investment and employment.

Effects of Unemployment

• Increases poverty.

• Reduces the standard of living.

• Leads to crime and social unrest.

• Reduces government tax revenue.

• Causes waste of human resources.

• Slows economic growth and development.

Measures to Reduce Unemployment

Promoting Industrialization – Establishing industries creates more jobs.

Encouraging Entrepreneurship – Supporting small businesses increases employment.

Improving Education and Skills – Vocational and technical training prepares people for available jobs.

Providing Loans – Access to finance helps individuals establish businesses.

Government Investment – Public projects create employment opportunities.

Maintaining Political Stability – A peaceful environment attracts investors and creates jobs.

Importance of Full Employment

• Increases national income.

• Reduces poverty.

• Improves the standard of living.

• Promotes economic growth.

• Reduces crime and social problems.

📈 CHAPTER 23: NATIONAL INCOME

National income refers to the total monetary value of all final goods and services produced by the citizens and businesses of a country within a given period, usually one year. It is used to measure the economic performance and standard of living of a nation.

Meaning of National Income

National income is the total income earned by the factors of production (land, labour, capital and entrepreneurship) in a country during a given period of time.

Concepts of National Income

Gross Domestic Product (GDP) – The total value of all final goods and services produced within a country's borders in one year.

Gross National Product (GNP) – The total value of all final goods and services produced by the citizens of a country, whether within or outside the country, in one year.

Net National Product (NNP) – Gross National Product minus depreciation.

National Income (NI) – The total income earned by all factors of production after adjusting for indirect taxes and subsidies.

Personal Income (PI) – The total income actually received by individuals before personal taxes are deducted.

Disposable Income (DI) – The income left for individuals to spend or save after paying personal taxes.

Methods of Measuring National Income

Output (Product) Method – Measures the total value of all final goods and services produced.

Income Method – Measures the total income earned by the factors of production.

Expenditure Method – Measures the total expenditure on final goods and services.

Importance of National Income

• Measures the economic performance of a country.

• Assists in national planning.

• Helps compare the standard of living between countries.

• Guides government economic policies.

• Assists investors in making business decisions.

• Helps determine economic growth.

Problems of Measuring National Income

Poor Record Keeping – Many economic activities are not properly recorded.

Subsistence Production – Goods produced for personal use are difficult to value.

Illegal Economic Activities – Smuggling and other illegal activities are usually excluded.

Double Counting – Counting intermediate goods more than once may overstate national income.

Inadequate Statistical Data – Reliable data may not always be available.

Uses of National Income Statistics

• Economic planning.

• Formulation of government policies.

• Comparison of economic performance.

• Determination of economic growth.

• Allocation of national resources.

Reasons for International Trade

Importance of ECOWAS

Economic development is the continuous improvement in the quality of life of the people through increased production, improved income, better education, healthcare and infrastructure.

Types of Economic Planning

Short-Term Plan – A development plan that covers one to three years.

Medium-Term Plan – A plan that usually covers four to seven years.

Long-Term Plan – A development plan that extends beyond ten years.

Characteristics of a Developing Economy

• Low per capita income.

• High unemployment.

• Heavy dependence on agriculture.

• Low level of industrialization.

• Rapid population growth.

• Poor infrastructure.

• High level of poverty.

Problems of Economic Planning

Inadequate Funding – Government may lack enough funds to implement projects.

Corruption – Mismanagement of public funds affects development.

Poor Data Collection – Inaccurate statistics lead to poor planning.

Political Instability – Frequent changes in government disrupt development plans.

Poor Implementation – Good plans may fail due to weak execution.

Importance of Economic Development

• Improves the standard of living.

• Reduces poverty.

• Creates employment opportunities.

• Promotes industrial and agricultural development.

• Improves healthcare and education.

• Encourages investment.

• Increases national income.

Exam Tips

⭐ Be able to explain the problems affecting economic planning and measures to overcome them.
