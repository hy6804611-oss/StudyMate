📂 Parts of Speech

Noun

A noun is the name of a person, place, animal, thing, or idea.

📖 Meaning: A noun gives a name to someone or something.

🎯 When to Use: Use a noun whenever you are naming a person, place, object, animal, or idea.

📝 Examples:

  • Person → Teacher
  • Place → Lagos
  • Animal → Lion
  • Thing → Book
  • Idea → Honesty

💡 Memory Tip: If you can name it, it is probably a noun.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Do not confuse nouns with adjectives. "Beautiful" describes a noun; it is not a noun.

Pronoun

A pronoun is a word used in place of a noun.

📖 Meaning: Pronouns prevent unnecessary repetition of nouns.

🎯 When to Use: Use pronouns after mentioning a noun to avoid repeating it.

📝 Examples:

  • Ahmed is a student. He studies every day.
  • Fatima bought a book. She likes reading.

💡 Memory Tip: Pronouns replace nouns.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Ensure the pronoun agrees with the noun it replaces.

Verb

A verb is a word that shows an action, occurrence, or state of being.

📖 Meaning: Every complete sentence must contain a verb.

🎯 When to Use: Use a verb to express what someone or something does or is.

📝 Examples:

  • The boy runs.
  • She writes neatly.
  • They are happy.

💡 Memory Tip: No verb, no complete sentence.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Do not confuse helping verbs with main verbs.

Adjective

An adjective is a word that describes or modifies a noun or pronoun.

📖 Meaning: Adjectives tell us more about people, places, animals, or things.

🎯 When to Use: Use adjectives to describe size, colour, shape, quality, number, or condition.

📝 Examples:

  • A beautiful girl.
  • A large house.
  • Three red apples.

💡 Memory Tip: Adjectives answer questions like "What kind?", "Which one?", and "How many?"

⚠️ Common Mistake: Do not use an adjective where an adverb is required.

📂 Tenses

Simple Present Tense

Subject + Base Verb (Verb₁) + Object

📖 Meaning: The Simple Present Tense describes habits, routines, facts, and general truths.

🎯 When to Use: Use it for daily activities, permanent situations, and universal facts.

📝 Examples:

  • She goes to school every day.
  • The sun rises in the east.
  • We play football every Saturday.

💡 Memory Tip: Add -s or -es to the verb when the subject is He, She, or It.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ He go to school.
✅ He goes to school.

Simple Past Tense

Subject + Past Verb (Verb₂) + Object

📖 Meaning: The Simple Past Tense describes actions that happened and finished in the past.

🎯 When to Use: Use it for completed actions such as yesterday, last week, or last year.

📝 Examples:

  • They visited Kano yesterday.
  • She wrote a letter.
  • The children played outside.

💡 Memory Tip: Regular verbs usually end with -ed, while irregular verbs have special forms.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ She goed home.
✅ She went home.

Simple Future Tense

Subject + Will + Base Verb + Object

📖 Meaning: The Simple Future Tense describes actions that will happen in the future.

🎯 When to Use: Use it for future plans, predictions, promises, and decisions.

📝 Examples:

  • I will travel tomorrow.
  • She will pass the examination.
  • They will arrive soon.

💡 Memory Tip: After will, always use the base form of the verb.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ She will goes home.
✅ She will go home.

Present Continuous Tense

Subject + am/is/are + Verb-ing + Object

📖 Meaning: The Present Continuous Tense describes actions happening now or around the present time.

🎯 When to Use: Use it for actions taking place at the moment of speaking or temporary activities.

📝 Examples:

  • She is reading a novel.
  • They are playing football.
  • I am studying English.

💡 Memory Tip: Always use am, is, or are before the -ing verb.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ She reading a book.
✅ She is reading a book.

📂 Subject–Verb Agreement (Concord)

Singular Subject + Singular Verb

Singular Subject → Singular Verb

📖 Meaning: A singular subject must always take a singular verb.

🎯 When to Use: Use this rule when the subject refers to one person, place, animal, or thing.

📝 Examples:

  • The boy plays football.
  • She writes neatly.
  • My father works in Abuja.

💡 Memory Tip: He, She, and It usually take verbs ending in -s or -es.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ The boy play football.
✅ The boy plays football.

Plural Subject + Plural Verb

Plural Subject → Plural Verb

📖 Meaning: A plural subject must take a plural verb.

🎯 When to Use: Use this rule when referring to more than one person, place, animal, or thing.

📝 Examples:

  • The boys play football.
  • They study every evening.
  • My friends live in Kaduna.

💡 Memory Tip: Plural subjects usually use the base form of the verb.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ The boys plays football.
✅ The boys play football.

Subjects Joined by "And"

Subject + and + Subject → Plural Verb

📖 Meaning: Two or more subjects joined by and usually take a plural verb.

🎯 When to Use: Use this rule when combining two people or things.

📝 Examples:

  • John and Mary are classmates.
  • The teacher and the students were present.
  • Rice and beans are my favourite meal.

💡 Memory Tip: "And" normally means more than one.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ John and Mary is here.
✅ John and Mary are here.

Subjects Joined by "Either...Or" / "Neither...Nor"

Verb agrees with the subject nearest to it.

📖 Meaning: When subjects are joined by either...or or neither...nor, the verb agrees with the subject closest to it.

🎯 When to Use: Use this rule for alternative or negative choices.

📝 Examples:

  • Either the teacher or the students are coming.
  • Either the students or the teacher is coming.
  • Neither Musa nor his friends are ready.

💡 Memory Tip: Always look at the subject nearest the verb.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Either the students or the teacher are coming.
✅ Either the students or the teacher is coming.

📂 Active and Passive Voice

Active Voice

Subject + Verb + Object

📖 Meaning: In the active voice, the subject performs the action.

🎯 When to Use: Use the active voice when you want your writing to be direct, clear, and easy to understand.

📝 Examples:

  • The teacher teaches English.
  • Mary wrote a letter.
  • The police arrested the thief.

💡 Memory Tip: The subject is the doer of the action.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ English teaches the teacher.
✅ The teacher teaches English.

Passive Voice

Object + be Verb + Past Participle + by + Subject

📖 Meaning: In the passive voice, the object of the active sentence becomes the subject.

🎯 When to Use: Use the passive voice when the action is more important than the person performing it or when the doer is unknown.

📝 Examples:

  • English is taught by the teacher.
  • A letter was written by Mary.
  • The thief was arrested by the police.

💡 Memory Tip: Passive voice always uses a form of be (is, am, are, was, were, been, being) together with the past participle.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ English taught by the teacher.
✅ English is taught by the teacher.

Changing Active Voice to Passive Voice

Object → be Verb → Past Participle → by → Subject

📖 Meaning: To change an active sentence into a passive sentence, move the object to the beginning and change the verb appropriately.

🎯 When to Use: Used in grammar exercises, examinations, and formal writing.

📝 Examples:

  • Active: The chef cooked the food.
  • Passive: The food was cooked by the chef.
  • Active: The students cleaned the classroom.
  • Passive: The classroom was cleaned by the students.

💡 Memory Tip: The object of the active sentence becomes the subject of the passive sentence.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Always use the correct form of the verb be based on the tense.

Changing Passive Voice to Active Voice

Subject + Verb + Object

📖 Meaning: To change a passive sentence back to active voice, identify the doer of the action and make it the subject.

🎯 When to Use: Used when making sentences more direct and natural.

📝 Examples:

  • Passive: The window was broken by Ali.
  • Active: Ali broke the window.
  • Passive: The assignment was completed by the students.
  • Active: The students completed the assignment.

💡 Memory Tip: Find the person after by; that person usually becomes the subject.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Do not forget to change the verb to match the new subject and tense.

📂 Direct and Indirect Speech

Direct Speech

Speaker's Exact Words Inside Quotation Marks (" ")

📖 Meaning: Direct speech reports the exact words spoken by a person.

🎯 When to Use: Use direct speech when quoting someone's exact words.

📝 Examples:

  • John said, "I am happy."
  • The teacher said, "Study hard."
  • Mary asked, "Where are you going?"

💡 Memory Tip: Direct speech always uses quotation marks (" ").

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ John said I am happy.
✅ John said, "I am happy."

Indirect Speech (Reported Speech)

Reporting Verb + That + Reported Statement

📖 Meaning: Indirect speech reports what someone said without using the exact words.

🎯 When to Use: Use indirect speech when reporting information rather than quoting directly.

📝 Examples:

  • John said that he was happy.
  • The teacher said that we should study hard.
  • Mary asked where I was going.

💡 Memory Tip: Remove quotation marks and adjust the tense when necessary.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ John said that I am happy.
✅ John said that he was happy.

Tense Changes in Indirect Speech

Present → Past
Past → Past Perfect
Will → Would
Can → Could
May → Might

📖 Meaning: When the reporting verb is in the past, the tense of the reported speech usually changes.

🎯 When to Use: Use these changes when converting direct speech into indirect speech.

📝 Examples:

  • "I am tired." → He said that he was tired.
  • "I will come." → She said that she would come.
  • "I can swim." → He said that he could swim.

💡 Memory Tip: Move the tense one step back if the reporting verb is in the past.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Do not change the tense if the statement expresses a universal truth.

Changes in Time and Place Expressions

Now → Then
Today → That day
Tomorrow → The next day
Yesterday → The previous day
Here → There

📖 Meaning: Time and place words often change when converting direct speech to indirect speech.

🎯 When to Use: Apply these changes when reporting speech in a different context.

📝 Examples:

  • "I will come tomorrow." → He said that he would come the next day.
  • "I live here." → She said that she lived there.

💡 Memory Tip: Think about the change in time and location from the speaker's perspective.

⚠️ Common Mistake: Do not leave words like "tomorrow" or "here" unchanged when the context requires a change.

📂 Sentence Structure

Basic Sentence Structure

Subject + Verb + Object (SVO)

📖 Meaning: A complete sentence usually consists of a subject, a verb, and an object.

🎯 When to Use: Use this structure to form simple and correct English sentences.

📝 Examples:

  • The boy kicked the ball.
  • Mary reads a book.
  • They played football.

💡 Memory Tip: Every complete sentence must have at least a subject and a verb.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Plays football.
✅ The boy plays football.

Simple Sentence

One Independent Clause

📖 Meaning: A simple sentence contains only one complete idea.

🎯 When to Use: Use it to express one complete thought clearly.

📝 Examples:

  • The baby is sleeping.
  • We study every evening.
  • Ali laughed loudly.

💡 Memory Tip: One subject and one main verb usually form a simple sentence.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Because I was tired.
✅ I was tired.

Compound Sentence

Independent Clause + Coordinating Conjunction + Independent Clause

📖 Meaning: A compound sentence joins two complete sentences using a coordinating conjunction.

🎯 When to Use: Use it to connect two related ideas of equal importance.

📝 Examples:

  • I studied hard, and I passed the examination.
  • She was tired, but she finished her work.
  • We hurried, so we arrived early.

💡 Memory Tip: Remember the conjunctions: For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So (FANBOYS).

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ I studied hard I passed the examination.
✅ I studied hard, and I passed the examination.

Complex Sentence

Independent Clause + Dependent Clause

📖 Meaning: A complex sentence contains one independent clause and at least one dependent clause.

🎯 When to Use: Use it to show relationships such as time, reason, condition, or contrast.

📝 Examples:

  • Although it was raining, we played football.
  • I stayed at home because I was sick.
  • If you study hard, you will succeed.

💡 Memory Tip: Words like because, although, if, when, since, while often introduce dependent clauses.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Because I was sick.
✅ I stayed at home because I was sick.

📂 Punctuation Rules

Full Stop (.)

Use a full stop (.) at the end of a complete statement.

📖 Meaning: A full stop marks the end of a declarative sentence.

🎯 When to Use: Use it after statements, facts, and completed thoughts.

📝 Examples:

  • She is a doctor.
  • We are going to school.
  • The meeting starts at 9 a.m.

💡 Memory Tip: Every complete statement should end with a full stop.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ She is a doctor
✅ She is a doctor.

Comma (,)

Use a comma (,) to separate items or ideas.

📖 Meaning: A comma separates words, phrases, or clauses to improve clarity.

🎯 When to Use: Use commas in lists, after introductory words, and before coordinating conjunctions joining two independent clauses.

📝 Examples:

  • I bought rice, beans, tomatoes, and onions.
  • After school, we went home.
  • She studied hard, so she passed.

💡 Memory Tip: A comma creates a short pause while reading.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ I bought rice beans tomatoes.
✅ I bought rice, beans, tomatoes, and onions.

Question Mark (?)

Use a question mark (?) at the end of a direct question.

📖 Meaning: A question mark shows that a sentence is asking a question.

🎯 When to Use: Use it after direct questions only.

📝 Examples:

  • Where are you going?
  • Did you finish your homework?
  • What is your name?

💡 Memory Tip: If you expect an answer, end the sentence with a question mark.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Where are you going.
✅ Where are you going?

Exclamation Mark (!)

Use an exclamation mark (!) to express strong feelings or commands.

📖 Meaning: An exclamation mark shows excitement, surprise, warning, or strong emotion.

🎯 When to Use: Use it for exclamations, warnings, greetings, and strong commands.

📝 Examples:

  • Congratulations!
  • Stop!
  • What a beautiful day!

💡 Memory Tip: Use only one exclamation mark in formal writing.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Congratulations!!!
✅ Congratulations!

📂 Capitalization Rules

Capitalize the First Word of a Sentence

First Word → Capital Letter

📖 Meaning: Every sentence must begin with a capital letter.

🎯 When to Use: Use this rule at the beginning of every new sentence.

📝 Examples:

  • The students are studying.
  • My father is a doctor.
  • We are going to Abuja.

💡 Memory Tip: Always check the first letter before ending your writing.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ the students are studying.
✅ The students are studying.

Capitalize Proper Nouns

Names of People, Places, Organizations and Specific Things → Capital Letters

📖 Meaning: Proper nouns always begin with capital letters.

🎯 When to Use: Use capital letters for names of people, countries, cities, schools, organizations and languages.

📝 Examples:

  • Ahmed
  • Nigeria
  • London
  • University of Lagos
  • English

💡 Memory Tip: If it is a unique name, capitalize it.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ nigeria
✅ Nigeria

Capitalize Days, Months and Holidays

Days, Months and Holidays → Capital Letters

📖 Meaning: The names of days, months and holidays always begin with capital letters.

🎯 When to Use: Use this rule whenever writing dates or mentioning special occasions.

📝 Examples:

  • Monday
  • January
  • Friday
  • Eid al-Fitr
  • Christmas

💡 Memory Tip: Days and months are always capitalized.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ monday, january
✅ Monday, January

Capitalize the Pronoun "I"

"I" is Always Written in Capital Form

📖 Meaning: The pronoun I must always be written as a capital letter, regardless of its position in a sentence.

🎯 When to Use: Use this rule whenever referring to yourself.

📝 Examples:

  • I am a student.
  • My brother and I went to school.
  • I will visit Kano tomorrow.

💡 Memory Tip: The word I is never written as i.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ i am happy.
✅ I am happy.

📂 Articles (A, An & The)

Indefinite Article - "A"

A + Singular Countable Noun Beginning with a Consonant Sound

📖 Meaning: A is used before a singular countable noun that begins with a consonant sound.

🎯 When to Use: Use a when referring to one person, animal, place, or thing for the first time.

📝 Examples:

  • A boy is playing.
  • She bought a pen.
  • He is a teacher.

💡 Memory Tip: Use a before consonant sounds, not just consonant letters.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ An book.
✅ A book.

Indefinite Article - "An"

An + Singular Countable Noun Beginning with a Vowel Sound

📖 Meaning: An is used before a singular countable noun that begins with a vowel sound.

🎯 When to Use: Use an before words that begin with the sounds a, e, i, o, or u.

📝 Examples:

  • An apple.
  • An egg.
  • An honest man.

💡 Memory Tip: Think about the sound, not the spelling. "Honest" begins with a vowel sound.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ A apple.
✅ An apple.

Definite Article - "The"

The + Specific Person, Place, Animal or Thing

📖 Meaning: The refers to a specific or already known person, place, animal, or thing.

🎯 When to Use: Use the when both the speaker and listener know what is being referred to.

📝 Examples:

  • The sun rises in the east.
  • Please close the door.
  • The teacher entered the classroom.

💡 Memory Tip: The points to something specific.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Please close door.
✅ Please close the door.

When Not to Use Articles

No Article Before Most Proper Nouns, Languages and Uncountable Nouns (General Meaning)

📖 Meaning: Some nouns do not need an article.

🎯 When to Use: Do not use articles before most names of people, countries, languages, subjects, or uncountable nouns when speaking generally.

📝 Examples:

  • Ahmed is my friend.
  • She speaks English.
  • Water is important for life.

💡 Memory Tip: Proper names usually do not need a, an, or the.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ The Ahmed is here.
✅ Ahmed is here.

📂 Prepositions

Preposition of Place

Preposition + Noun/Pronoun

📖 Meaning: A preposition of place shows the position or location of a person, animal, place, or thing.

🎯 When to Use: Use it to describe where someone or something is located.

📝 Examples:

  • The book is on the table.
  • The cat is under the chair.
  • She is inside the classroom.

💡 Memory Tip: Place prepositions answer the question "Where?"

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ The book is in the table.
✅ The book is on the table.

Preposition of Time

at → Specific Time
on → Days and Dates
in → Months, Years and Long Periods

📖 Meaning: Prepositions of time show when an action happens.

🎯 When to Use: Use the correct preposition depending on the time expression.

📝 Examples:

  • The class starts at 8:00 a.m.
  • We have English on Monday.
  • She was born in 2008.

💡 Memory Tip: At = Time, On = Day/Date, In = Month/Year.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ I was born on 2010.
✅ I was born in 2010.

Preposition of Direction

towards • into • onto • through • across • to

📖 Meaning: Prepositions of direction show movement from one place to another.

🎯 When to Use: Use them to describe movement or travel.

📝 Examples:

  • She walked into the room.
  • They ran across the road.
  • We travelled to Abuja.

💡 Memory Tip: Direction prepositions answer the question "Where to?"

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ She entered into the room quickly. (In many contexts, "entered the room" is more natural.)
✅ She entered the room.

Preposition After Certain Verbs

Depend on • Believe in • Listen to • Apologize for • Agree with

📖 Meaning: Some verbs are naturally followed by specific prepositions.

🎯 When to Use: Use the correct verb–preposition combination in speaking and writing.

📝 Examples:

  • I depend on my parents.
  • She believes in herself.
  • Please listen to the teacher.
  • He apologized for being late.

💡 Memory Tip: Learn common verb and preposition combinations together.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Listen the teacher.
✅ Listen to the teacher.

📂 Conjunctions

Coordinating Conjunctions

FANBOYS = For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So

📖 Meaning: Coordinating conjunctions join words, phrases, or independent clauses of equal importance.

🎯 When to Use: Use them to connect ideas that have equal grammatical value.

📝 Examples:

  • I studied hard, so I passed.
  • Ali and Musa are friends.
  • She wanted to travel, but she was ill.

💡 Memory Tip: Remember the word FANBOYS to recall all coordinating conjunctions.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ I was tired but I finished my work.
✅ I was tired, but I finished my work.

Subordinating Conjunctions

because, although, since, if, unless, while, when, before, after

📖 Meaning: Subordinating conjunctions connect a dependent clause to an independent clause.

🎯 When to Use: Use them to show reason, time, condition, contrast, or purpose.

📝 Examples:

  • Because it was raining, we stayed indoors.
  • If you study hard, you will succeed.
  • Although she was tired, she completed her assignment.

💡 Memory Tip: A subordinating conjunction usually introduces a dependent clause.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Because I was sick.
✅ I stayed at home because I was sick.

Correlative Conjunctions

either...or • neither...nor • both...and • not only...but also

📖 Meaning: Correlative conjunctions work in pairs to join equal words, phrases, or clauses.

🎯 When to Use: Use them to emphasize relationships between two ideas.

📝 Examples:

  • Either Ahmed or Musa will represent the class.
  • Neither the teacher nor the students were late.
  • Both English and Mathematics are compulsory subjects.
  • She is not only intelligent but also hardworking.

💡 Memory Tip: Correlative conjunctions always come in pairs.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Either Ahmed and Musa will come.
✅ Either Ahmed or Musa will come.

Choosing the Correct Conjunction

Reason → because
Contrast → but, although
Choice → or, either...or
Addition → and
Result → so

📖 Meaning: Different conjunctions express different relationships between ideas.

🎯 When to Use: Choose the conjunction that best matches the meaning of your sentence.

📝 Examples:

  • I was hungry, so I ate.
  • She studied hard because she had an examination.
  • He is poor but honest.

💡 Memory Tip: Think about the relationship between the two ideas before choosing a conjunction.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ I stayed home and it was raining.
✅ I stayed home because it was raining.

📂 Pronouns

Personal Pronouns

Personal Pronouns replace the names of people, places, animals, or things.

📖 Meaning: Personal pronouns are used instead of nouns to avoid unnecessary repetition.

🎯 When to Use: Use them when referring to someone or something already mentioned.

📝 Examples:

  • Ahmed is a student. He studies every day.
  • Mary is my friend. She is kind.
  • The children are playing. They are happy.

💡 Memory Tip: Personal pronouns replace nouns.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Ahmed is a student. Ahmed studies every day.
✅ Ahmed is a student. He studies every day.

Possessive Pronouns

mine • yours • his • hers • ours • theirs

📖 Meaning: Possessive pronouns show ownership without repeating the noun.

🎯 When to Use: Use them to show that something belongs to someone.

📝 Examples:

  • This bag is mine.
  • The blue car is theirs.
  • The victory was ours.

💡 Memory Tip: Possessive pronouns never need a noun after them.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ This book is my.
✅ This book is mine.

Reflexive Pronouns

myself • yourself • himself • herself • itself • ourselves • yourselves • themselves

📖 Meaning: Reflexive pronouns refer back to the subject of the sentence.

🎯 When to Use: Use them when the subject and the object are the same person or thing.

📝 Examples:

  • I taught myself HTML.
  • She hurt herself.
  • They introduced themselves.

💡 Memory Tip: Reflexive pronouns always end in -self or -selves.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Myself went to school.
✅ I went to school.

Demonstrative Pronouns

this • that • these • those

📖 Meaning: Demonstrative pronouns point to specific people or things.

🎯 When to Use: Use them to indicate whether something is near or far, singular or plural.

📝 Examples:

  • This is my phone.
  • Those are my classmates.
  • These belong to me.

💡 Memory Tip: This/These are for things nearby, while That/Those are for things farther away.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ This are my books.
✅ These are my books.

📂 Adjectives

Descriptive (Quality) Adjectives

Adjective + Noun

📖 Meaning: Descriptive adjectives tell us the quality, size, colour, shape, age, or appearance of a noun.

🎯 When to Use: Use them to describe people, places, animals, or things.

📝 Examples:

  • A beautiful girl
  • A tall building
  • A red car

💡 Memory Tip: Descriptive adjectives answer the question "What kind?"

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ She is a beauty girl.
✅ She is a beautiful girl.

Possessive Adjectives

my • your • his • her • its • our • their

📖 Meaning: Possessive adjectives show ownership and always come before a noun.

🎯 When to Use: Use them to indicate who owns something.

📝 Examples:

  • My book is on the table.
  • Her bag is new.
  • Their house is beautiful.

💡 Memory Tip: A possessive adjective is always followed by a noun.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ This book is my.
✅ This is my book.

Demonstrative Adjectives

this • that • these • those

📖 Meaning: Demonstrative adjectives point to specific nouns.

🎯 When to Use: Use them to identify people or things based on distance or number.

📝 Examples:

  • This book is interesting.
  • Those students are hardworking.
  • These apples are fresh.

💡 Memory Tip: This/These = Near, That/Those = Far.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ This books are mine.
✅ These books are mine.

Comparative and Superlative Adjectives

Positive → Comparative → Superlative Tall → Taller → Tallest

📖 Meaning: Comparative adjectives compare two people or things, while superlative adjectives compare three or more.

🎯 When to Use: Use comparative for two items and superlative for three or more.

📝 Examples:

  • Ali is taller than Musa.
  • John is the tallest student in the class.
  • This road is wider than the other one.

💡 Memory Tip: -er compares two; -est compares three or more.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ He is more taller than me.
✅ He is taller than me.

Verb + Adverb

📖 Meaning: Adverbs of manner describe how an action is performed.

🎯 When to Use: Use them to explain the way someone or something performs an action.

Adverbs of Time

⚠️ Common Mistake:

Verb + Place Adverb

📂 Essay Writing Guide

Basic Essay Structure

Introduction → Body Paragraphs → Conclusion

📖 Meaning: A good essay has three main parts: an introduction, body paragraphs, and a conclusion.

🎯 When to Use: Use this structure for narrative, descriptive, argumentative, expository, and letter-writing essays.

📝 Examples:

  • Introduction: Introduce the topic.
  • Body: Explain your ideas with examples.
  • Conclusion: Summarize your main points.

💡 Memory Tip: Think of an essay as Beginning → Middle → End.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Writing without paragraphs.
✅ Organize your essay into clear paragraphs.

Writing an Introduction

Hook → Background Information → Thesis Statement

📖 Meaning: The introduction attracts the reader and explains the purpose of the essay.

🎯 When to Use: Use it at the beginning of every essay.

📝 Examples:

  • Education is the foundation of national development.
  • Technology has changed the way people communicate.

💡 Memory Tip: A strong introduction makes the reader want to continue reading.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Starting the essay without introducing the topic.
✅ Begin with an interesting opening sentence.

Writing Body Paragraphs

Topic Sentence → Explanation → Example → Closing Sentence

📖 Meaning: Each body paragraph should discuss one main idea with supporting details.

🎯 When to Use: Use this format for every paragraph in the body of the essay.

📝 Examples:

  • Main Idea: Reading improves knowledge.
  • Explanation: Books expose readers to new ideas.
  • Example: Students who read regularly perform better academically.

💡 Memory Tip: One paragraph = One main idea.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Mixing several unrelated ideas in one paragraph.
✅ Focus on one idea per paragraph.

Writing a Conclusion

Restate Main Idea → Summarize Key Points → Final Thought

📖 Meaning: The conclusion brings the essay to a proper ending without introducing new ideas.

🎯 When to Use: Use it to end every essay clearly and effectively.

📝 Examples:

  • In conclusion, education is the key to success.
  • Therefore, everyone should develop good reading habits.

💡 Memory Tip: End strongly by reminding the reader of your main message.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Introducing a new argument in the conclusion.
✅ Only summarize and conclude your essay.

📂 Letter Writing

Informal Letter Format

Sender's Address → Date → Salutation → Introduction → Body → Conclusion → Closing → Signature

📖 Meaning: An informal letter is written to friends, parents, siblings, or relatives using a friendly tone.

🎯 When to Use: Use it when writing to someone you know personally.

📝 Examples:

  • A letter to your friend.
  • A letter to your parents.
  • A letter to your brother or sister.

💡 Memory Tip: Informal letters are friendly and conversational.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Using "Dear Sir" when writing to your friend.
✅ Use "Dear Ahmed," or "Dear Fatima,".

Formal Letter Format

Sender's Address → Date → Recipient's Address → Salutation → Subject → Body → Closing → Signature

📖 Meaning: A formal letter is written to schools, companies, government offices, or other organizations using a polite and professional tone.

🎯 When to Use: Use it for applications, complaints, requests, and official communication.

📝 Examples:

  • Application for admission.
  • Application for employment.
  • Letter of complaint.

💡 Memory Tip: Formal letters should be polite, respectful, and straight to the point.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Hi Principal,
✅ Dear Sir/Madam, or Dear Principal,

Salutations and Closings

Informal → Dear John, ... Yours sincerely,
Formal → Dear Sir/Madam, ... Yours faithfully,

📖 Meaning: Every letter begins with a salutation and ends with an appropriate closing.

🎯 When to Use: Choose the correct greeting and closing based on the type of letter.

📝 Examples:

  • Dear Musa, ... Yours sincerely,
  • Dear Sir/Madam, ... Yours faithfully,
  • Dear Principal, ... Yours faithfully,

💡 Memory Tip: If you know the person's name, use Yours sincerely. If you do not know the person's name, use Yours faithfully.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Dear Sir ... Yours sincerely.
✅ Dear Sir ... Yours faithfully.

Writing Tips for Letters

Be Clear → Be Polite → Be Organized → Check Grammar and Spelling

📖 Meaning: Good letters are easy to read, well organized, and free from grammar and spelling mistakes.

🎯 When to Use: Apply these tips to every letter you write.

📝 Examples:

  • Write one main idea in each paragraph.
  • Use polite language throughout the letter.
  • Proofread before submitting.

💡 Memory Tip: Read your letter once before sending it to correct mistakes.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Writing without paragraphs or punctuation.
✅ Organize your letter into clear paragraphs and use correct punctuation.

📂 Comprehension Tips

Read the Passage Carefully

Read → Understand → Answer

📖 Meaning: Read the entire passage carefully before attempting any question.

🎯 When to Use: Use this method during comprehension exercises and examinations.

📝 Examples:

  • Read the passage from beginning to end.
  • Identify the main idea before answering questions.
  • Do not rush.

💡 Memory Tip: Read twice if necessary before answering.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Answering questions before reading the passage.
✅ Read the passage first.

Identify the Main Idea

Main Idea = Central Message of the Passage

📖 Meaning: Every comprehension passage has one central idea the writer wants to communicate.

🎯 When to Use: Use this skill to answer summary and main idea questions.

📝 Examples:

  • Find what most paragraphs discuss.
  • Ignore unnecessary details.

💡 Memory Tip: Ask yourself: "What is the passage mainly about?"

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Choosing a minor detail as the main idea.
✅ Choose the overall message.

Use Context Clues

Unknown Word → Read the Surrounding Sentences

📖 Meaning: The meaning of unfamiliar words can often be understood from nearby words or sentences.

🎯 When to Use: Use this method when vocabulary questions appear.

📝 Examples:

  • Look at the sentence before and after the unfamiliar word.
  • Identify hints given by the writer.

💡 Memory Tip: The passage usually gives clues to the meaning.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Guessing without reading the surrounding sentences.
✅ Use context before deciding.

Answer Using Evidence from the Passage

Answer = Information Supported by the Passage

📖 Meaning: Your answers should be based on what is written in the passage, not on personal opinion.

🎯 When to Use: Use this rule for all comprehension questions.

📝 Examples:

  • Underline important facts while reading.
  • Quote or refer to relevant parts of the passage where necessary.

💡 Memory Tip: The correct answer is usually found in the passage.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Answering from personal experience.
✅ Answer using information provided in the passage.

🎯 When to Use: Use synonyms to avoid repetition and improve your writing and speaking.

📂 Common Grammar Mistakes

Subject–Verb Agreement Errors

Singular Subject → Singular Verb
Plural Subject → Plural Verb

📖 Meaning: The verb must always agree with the subject in number.

🎯 When to Use: Use this rule in every sentence you write or speak.

📝 Examples:

  • ✅ She plays football.
  • ✅ They play football.
  • ✅ The boy is happy.

💡 Memory Tip: He, She, It usually take verbs ending in -s in the simple present tense.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ She play football.
✅ She plays football.

Confusing Their, There and They're

Their = Possession
There = Place
They're = They are

📖 Meaning: These three words sound alike but have different meanings.

🎯 When to Use: Choose the correct word based on the meaning of the sentence.

📝 Examples:

  • Their books are on the table.
  • We are going there.
  • They're coming tomorrow.

💡 Memory Tip: They're = They are. If "they are" fits, use they're.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ There house is beautiful.
✅ Their house is beautiful.

Confusing Your and You're

Your = Shows Possession
You're = You are

📖 Meaning: These words have different meanings despite similar pronunciation.

🎯 When to Use: Use your to show ownership and you're to mean "you are."

📝 Examples:

  • Your bag is new.
  • You're a brilliant student.

💡 Memory Tip: If you can replace it with you are, use you're.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ Your welcome.
✅ You're welcome.

Double Negatives

Avoid Using Two Negative Words in One Sentence

📖 Meaning: Standard English usually avoids using two negatives to express one negative idea.

🎯 When to Use: Use only one negative word unless the context specifically requires otherwise.

📝 Examples:

  • ✅ I don't know anything.
  • ✅ She has never been there.

💡 Memory Tip: One negative is enough in standard English.

⚠️ Common Mistake:
❌ I don't know nothing.
✅ I don't know anything.
